📨 DAVAR LECHEM – Yom Revi'i, Tishi'i 8, 6028 AA

Shalom, Mitspacha!

⭐ THEME

Justice – resisting assimilation

📘 READING

“Do not do as they do in the land of Mitsrayim, where you dwelt, and do not do as they do in the land of Kena'an, where I am bringing you. Do not walk in their laws. Do My Right-Rulings and guard My Laws, to walk in them. I am YHVH your Elohim.”
- Wayyiqra (Leviticus) 18:3–4

“Guard yourself that you are not ensnared to follow them, after they are destroyed from before you, and that you do not inquire about their mighty ones, saying, ‘How did these nations serve their mighty ones? And let me do so too.’ You do not do so to YHVH your Elohim.”
- Devarim (Deuteronomy) 12:30–31

🟫 MITSVOT FOCUS

Assimilation occurs when the people of YHVH gradually adopt the values, customs, attitudes and practices of the surrounding nations until the distinction between covenant obedience and worldly conformity becomes difficult to recognise. It is not always a deliberate decision to abandon the Way. Sometimes it begins with small compromises, accepted because they are familiar, popular or convenient.

Wayyiqra gives a clear instruction: YHVH's people were not to imitate the practices of the nations among whom they had lived or the nations whose land they were entering. They were to follow His Right-Rulings and guard His Laws. Their conduct was to be shaped by the instructions of YHVH, not by the customs of the societies around them.

Devarim warns against becoming fascinated by the worship practices of other nations and then attempting to incorporate those practices into the worship of YHVH. Sincerity alone does not make a practice acceptable. We cannot decide that something is suitable for the worship of Elohim simply because other people have practised it for generations or because it appears meaningful to us. YHVH has the authority to establish how He is to be honoured.

Resisting assimilation requires discernment. Not every custom, language, form of clothing or ordinary cultural practice is automatically contrary to Torah. We must distinguish between what is simply part of everyday life and what compromises obedience, replaces YHVH's instructions or introduces practices He has not commanded. Our decisions must be guided by the Word rather than by fear, prejudice or personal preference.

We must also recognise that assimilation can take place in our thinking. The pursuit of popularity, wealth, status and acceptance can gradually influence our priorities. When the standards of the surrounding world become our measure of success, we may begin to excuse dishonesty, disregard responsibility, tolerate injustice or place personal comfort above faithfulness.

The call to resist assimilation is not a call to treat other people with contempt. We are to remain just, compassionate and respectful in our dealings with everyone. Our distinction must be demonstrated through obedience and integrity, not arrogance or hostility. We can live among the nations without allowing their standards to determine our covenant conduct.

Justice is also involved because the consequences of assimilation extend beyond the individual. When families and communities abandon righteous standards, the vulnerable can suffer, responsibilities can be neglected and wrongdoing can become normalised. Upholding the instructions of YHVH protects not only our own walk but also the integrity of our households and communities.

Today, let us examine what has influenced our beliefs, habits and decisions. We must be willing to recognise and correct whatever has drawn us away from the instructions of YHVH. Our loyalty belongs to Him, and our lives must reflect the standards He has established.

🩵 REFLECTION

Have I allowed the customs or values of the surrounding world to influence my understanding of what is acceptable before YHVH?

Do I examine inherited traditions and familiar practices against the Word, or do I accept them simply because they have always been part of my environment?

Can I distinguish between ordinary cultural differences and practices that compromise obedience or introduce forms of worship that YHVH has not commanded?

Where might the desire for acceptance, comfort or recognition be weakening my commitment to the instructions of YHVH?

Does my conduct demonstrate justice, honesty and compassion, even when the surrounding society accepts different standards?

What influence or practice do I need to examine more carefully today so that my walk remains faithful to YHVH?

Let us remember that belonging to YHVH requires more than a different name or outward identity. It requires a life directed by His Word. May we resist assimilation without losing compassion, uphold justice without becoming self-righteous and remain faithful without seeking the approval of the world.

👐 PRAYER

YHVH our Elohim, we thank You for Your instructions and for the clear direction You have given Your people. You have called us to walk in Your Ways and to guard Your Commands. Help us to recognise where the influence of the surrounding world has entered our thinking, our homes and our daily conduct.

Give us discernment to examine the customs and traditions we have inherited. Teach us to distinguish between ordinary cultural practices and those that conflict with Your Word or compromise the worship due to You alone. Keep us from accepting something merely because it is popular, familiar or widely practised.

Strengthen us to resist pressure to conform when conformity would require disobedience. Remove from us the fear of rejection and the desire for human approval. Give us courage to correct our ways whenever Your Word reveals that we have departed from Your instructions.

Guard our families and communities against influences that weaken faithfulness, undermine responsibility or normalise injustice. Give parents and elders wisdom to teach the younger generation to examine everything carefully and to choose obedience over popularity. Let our homes be places where Your instructions are respected and practised.

Teach us to remain distinct without becoming proud, hostile or unfair toward others. Let justice, compassion, honesty and self-control be evident in our dealings with all people. May we never use our commitment to Your Ways as an excuse to mistreat anyone.

Protect those who experience pressure, ridicule or rejection because they refuse to compromise their convictions. Give them strength to remain steadfast, wisdom in their responses and peace in their hearts. Help us to encourage one another rather than allowing fear or outside pressure to divide us.

May our loyalty to You remain greater than our desire for acceptance. Keep our steps directed by Your Word, and let our lives reflect the righteousness, justice and faithfulness You require of us. Through Yahusha HaMashiach, our Master and Redeemer.

Baruch YHVH. Emet.
