THEME

Guarding reverent speech

READING

“Let the words of my mouth and the meditation of my heart be acceptable before You, O YHVH, my Rock and my Redeemer.”
- Tehillim (Psalms) 19:14

“Let no corrupt word proceed out of your mouth, but only what is good for the building up of the need, that it might give favour to those who hear.”
- Ephesians 4:29

“A soft answer turns away wrath, but a harsh word stirs up anger.”
- Mishlei (Proverbs) 15:1

MITSVOT FOCUS

Yesterday we considered the prohibition of blasphemy and the seriousness of misrepresenting the Name of YHVH. Today we continue by examining how we guard our speech so that it remains reverent, truthful and pleasing before Him.

Reverent speech is not limited to avoiding certain expressions. It is the discipline of recognising that our words carry responsibility. What we say can honour YHVH, strengthen another person, bring understanding and establish peace. Our words can also wound, mislead, provoke anger or bring dishonour to the One we claim to serve.

The words of Tehillim teach us to examine both our spoken words and the thoughts from which they arise. Speech begins in the heart and mind. When resentment, pride, jealousy or impatience are allowed to grow within us, they often find their way into our conversations. Guarding our speech therefore requires more than controlling our tongues; it requires allowing YHVH to correct our inner attitudes.

The instruction in Ephesians reminds us that our words should build up rather than corrupt. This does not mean that every conversation must be pleasant or that we should avoid difficult truths. Righteous correction, warning and honest disagreement have their proper place. Yet even correction must serve a purpose: to bring truth, understanding and restoration where possible. Words spoken merely to humiliate or destroy do not reflect the character of YHVH.

A reverent person also learns when to remain silent. We do not need to answer every accusation, repeat every rumour or participate in every argument. Sometimes restraint protects peace and prevents unnecessary harm. At other times, silence may become neglect when truth or the vulnerable need to be defended. Discernment is required to know when to speak, what to say and how to say it.

Mishlei teaches that a gentle answer can turn away wrath. Gentleness is not weakness, and a calm response does not mean surrendering truth. It means refusing to allow another person's anger to govern our own conduct. We can speak firmly without becoming abusive, and we can stand for righteousness without allowing pride or hostility to control our words.

Guarding reverent speech also means being careful with the words we speak about YHVH. We should not use His Name casually, make careless claims about His will, or present our personal opinions as His instructions. We must distinguish what Scripture says from our own understanding and be willing to correct ourselves when we have spoken inaccurately.

Let us therefore make our speech an offering of obedience. May our words be truthful, measured, gracious and purposeful. May we speak in a way that honours YHVH, strengthens those who hear us and reflects the righteousness we seek to walk in.

REFLECTION

What is the condition of our heart when we speak? Do our words reveal patience, humility and reverence, or do they expose resentment, pride and impatience?

When we correct someone, is our purpose to help them understand and return to what is right, or are we trying to win an argument or make them feel small?

Do we know when to speak and when to remain silent? Are we guarding peace without neglecting truth, and standing for truth without allowing anger to rule us?

Let us examine our words today. May every conversation become an opportunity to honour YHVH through truth, wisdom, restraint and kindness.

PRAYER

YHVH our Elohim, we come before You with gratitude for the instruction You have given us concerning our words. Teach us to guard our speech and to recognise the responsibility carried by every word we speak.

Search our hearts and reveal the attitudes that produce careless, harsh or harmful speech. Forgive us for words spoken in anger, pride, impatience or contempt. Help us to make right what we have damaged and to seek forgiveness when we have wounded others.

Guide us by Your Ru'ach HaQodesh. Give us wisdom to know when to speak, when to listen and when silence is the righteous choice. Teach us to correct with humility, to defend truth with courage and to answer anger with self-control.

May our words bring understanding rather than confusion, encouragement rather than humiliation, and peace rather than needless conflict. Let our speech be truthful and gracious, and may it reflect the character of YHVH whom we serve.

May the words of our mouths and the meditations of our hearts be acceptable before You, our Rock and our Redeemer. Strengthen us to honour Your Name not only in what we proclaim, but in every conversation and every choice.

In the Name of Yahusha HaMashiach, we pray.

Baruch YHVH. Emet.
