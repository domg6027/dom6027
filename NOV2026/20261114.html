📖 MESSAGE

Respecting created roles

YHVH created humanity with intention, distinction and purpose. The account of creation establishes that humanity was created in the image of Elohim, male and female. These distinctions are not accidental or meaningless. They form part of the order established by the Creator.

The Torah gives specific instructions concerning the distinction between male and female. Devarim 22:5 prohibits a woman from wearing that which pertains to a man and a man from putting on a woman's garment. This instruction must be taken seriously because it is part of the revealed Torah of YHVH.

However, respecting this command requires understanding it responsibly. Clothing styles, customs and social expectations have varied across cultures and throughout history. A garment does not necessarily communicate the same thing in every society or period. We must therefore distinguish the command itself from assumptions or additional restrictions created by human tradition.

The principle of respecting created roles extends beyond outward appearance. YHVH gives human beings responsibilities, and those responsibilities must be approached with integrity. We should not confuse the dignity of a person with the responsibilities or distinctions established by the Creator. Nor should we use those distinctions as an excuse for arrogance, domination, humiliation or injustice.

Every person remains accountable to YHVH for their own conduct. Our responsibility is to understand His instructions, apply them faithfully and avoid adding rules that He has not commanded. We must also resist the temptation to replace His standards with whatever happens to be popular or socially acceptable.

Justice and humility

The weekly theme brings an important balance to this understanding. Through Amos, YHVH confronts religious activity that is separated from righteousness. The prophet's message makes clear that outward expressions of worship cannot compensate for injustice. YHVH requires conduct that reflects His standards.

Micah reinforces this principle by identifying what YHVH requires: doing what is right, loving kindness and walking humbly with Him. Justice is not simply the ability to identify what another person has done wrong. It also requires us to examine our own behaviour, motives and use of authority.

Humility is essential because knowledge of Torah must never become an excuse for pride. We may know what a command says and still fail to apply it with wisdom. We may defend a correct principle while treating another person unjustly. We may speak about obedience while refusing to examine our own conduct.

Respecting created roles therefore requires both conviction and responsibility. We must neither disregard YHVH's distinctions nor use them to justify unfair treatment. We must not confuse human customs with divine commands, and we must not make personal assumptions into universal laws.

The same standard of justice applies to everyone. Authority does not remove accountability. Knowledge does not excuse arrogance. Position does not give anyone permission to exploit another person. Each of us must remain willing to receive correction and to examine whether our actions reflect the instructions we profess to uphold.

This Shabbat, let us consider whether we respect the order established by YHVH while also walking in the justice and humility He requires. Let our obedience be sincere, our treatment of others fair, and our understanding guided by His Word rather than by pride or human tradition.

May we honour the Creator through the way we live, respect the responsibilities He has established and walk humbly before Him.

🩵 REFLECTION

Do I respect the distinctions and responsibilities established by YHVH, or do I allow personal preferences and cultural expectations to override His instructions?

Do I distinguish carefully between what YHVH has commanded and additional rules created by human tradition?

Do I treat others with dignity and fairness, even when I disagree with their choices or conduct?

Have I ever used my knowledge of Scripture to elevate myself, humiliate another person or avoid examining my own behaviour?

Do I pursue justice consistently, or do I apply standards differently depending on who is involved?

Am I willing to walk humbly before YHVH, receive correction and change where my conduct does not reflect His Word?

🤲🏻 CLOSING PRAYER

YHVH our Elohim, thank You for Your Word and for the order You established in creation. Thank You for teaching us that obedience must be joined with justice, kindness and humility.

Help us to respect the distinctions and responsibilities You have established. Give us wisdom to understand Your instructions correctly, without adding human traditions or allowing worldly expectations to replace Your standards. Teach us to recognise our own responsibilities and to walk faithfully in them.

Forgive us where pride, ignorance, prejudice or personal preference has influenced our decisions. Where we have treated others unfairly, show us what must be corrected. Where we have judged without understanding, teach us restraint. Where we have neglected our responsibilities, strengthen us to act with integrity.

We pray that You strengthen families to build relationships founded upon honesty, faithfulness, respect and responsible conduct. Protect marriages from betrayal and households from deception, manipulation and division.

Give parents wisdom to teach their children the value of truth and loyalty. Help children grow in discernment, integrity and respect for the responsibilities entrusted to them.

Protect Your people among the nations. Give wisdom to those who hold positions of authority, and restrain those who seek to use power for oppression, deception or destruction.

Strengthen those who must make difficult decisions for the protection of others. Give them the courage of Esther, the loyalty of Mordekai and the wisdom to act at the right time.

We pray for peace in the Middle East and for the protection of innocent people affected by war and violence. Strengthen and comfort the elderly, protect farmers and those whose livelihoods depend upon the land, and watch over people facing war, disaster and displacement.

Strengthen and protect evangelists, teachers and missionaries who faithfully proclaim Your Word. Give them wisdom, protection and endurance in the work entrusted to them.

Bring healing and comfort to those who are ill, suffering or afraid. Prepare Your people for the coming tribulation and teach us to remain faithful under pressure. Give us strength to stand firm in truth, even when obedience becomes difficult.

Bless and strengthen Derech Olam Mishkan International. Help us to serve faithfully, preserve truth, encourage Your people and remain steadfast in Your covenant. Give us wisdom in every responsibility and keep our work aligned with Your will.

As we enter this Shabbat, teach us to rest in Your provision, renew our strength and remember that justice, kindness and humility must remain part of our daily walk.

May our lives honour You, our Creator, and may our obedience be sincere before You.

Baruch YHVH. Emet.
