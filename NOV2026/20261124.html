THEME

Justice – faith over fear

READING

“Be strong and courageous, do not fear nor be afraid of them. For it is YHVH your Elohim who is going with you. He shall not leave you nor forsake you.”
- Devarim (Deuteronomy) 31:6

“When I am afraid, I trust in You.
In Elohim, whose word I praise, in Elohim I have trusted; I do not fear. What would flesh do to me?”
- Tehillim (Psalms) 56:3–4

“For Elohim has not given us a spirit of cowardice, but of power and of love and of self-control.”
- Timotiyos Bet (2 Timothy) 1:7

MITSVOT FOCUS

Fear can influence the decisions we make, the words we speak and the way we respond when we are confronted with injustice. It can cause us to remain silent when truth needs to be spoken, to tolerate wrongdoing because challenging it feels uncomfortable, or to compromise what is right in order to protect our own position. Yet the call to walk in justice cannot be separated from trust in YHVH. Faith over fear means that our obedience must be governed by His instructions rather than by intimidation, threats or the desire for human approval.

When YHVH instructed Yisra'el to be strong and courageous, He did not tell them that they would never encounter opposition. He reminded them that He would go with them and would not abandon them. Their courage was therefore not based on their own strength or their ability to control every outcome. It was grounded in the faithfulness and presence of YHVH. The same principle applies to our walk: we do not need to know every outcome before we obey what He has commanded.

Justice requires moral courage. It is easier to speak about righteousness when there is no personal cost, but our commitment is tested when truth may bring rejection, conflict or loss. We must not allow fear of powerful people, social pressure or personal consequences to make us participants in wrongdoing. Nor should we allow the fear of being disliked to prevent us from correcting error with wisdom, fairness and humility.

Faith over fear does not mean acting recklessly, provoking conflict or confusing aggression with courage. Scripture calls us to discernment and self-control. We must consider our words, examine our motives and act in a manner consistent with the instructions of YHVH. Courage without wisdom can become harmful; silence motivated by fear can become complicity. We are called to stand for what is right without becoming unjust ourselves.

The words of Tehillim remind us that fear may arise, but it does not have to rule us. The psalmist does not pretend that frightening circumstances do not exist. He chooses to place his trust in YHVH while facing them. This is an important distinction. Faith is not the denial of danger or uncertainty; it is the decision to trust YHVH above the circumstances that threaten to overwhelm us.

The Renewed Covenant reading also reminds us that the Ru'ach given by Elohim is not one of cowardice. Power, love and self-control belong together. Power must not become domination. Love must not become an excuse for tolerating injustice. Self-control must guide our response so that we do not repay wrongdoing with wrongdoing. These qualities enable us to remain steadfast while treating others with dignity and fairness.

We must also remember that justice belongs to YHVH. We are responsible for obeying His instructions, speaking truthfully, protecting those who are vulnerable and refusing to participate in oppression. We are not authorised to take vengeance into our own hands or to use righteousness as a reason for cruelty. Our confidence rests in the righteous Judge who sees what is hidden and judges without partiality.

Therefore, let us not surrender truth because we are afraid of consequences. Let us not confuse peace with silence in the face of wrongdoing, or courage with hostility. May our faith produce steady obedience, our love preserve compassion, and our self-control keep our conduct upright. When fear rises, let us remember who walks with us and continue doing what is right before YHVH.

REFLECTION

When fear influences our decisions, do we still choose what is right before YHVH, or do we allow the possibility of rejection, conflict or personal loss to determine our conduct?

Are there situations in which we remain silent about wrongdoing because speaking truth may be uncomfortable? How can we respond with courage, wisdom and fairness without becoming harsh or unjust ourselves?

Do we understand that faith over fear is not reckless behaviour, but trust in YHVH that enables us to obey even when the outcome is uncertain?

Let us examine whether power, love and self-control are visible in our responses to difficult situations. May we never use fear as an excuse for disobedience, or use our commitment to justice as an excuse to mistreat others. Our task is to remain faithful to YHVH and leave judgement in His hands.

PRAYER

YHVH our Elohim, we thank You that You are faithful and that You do not abandon those who trust in You. Teach us to place our confidence in Your Word rather than in our own strength, the approval of people or the circumstances around us.

When fear rises within us, strengthen our hearts to remain obedient. Give us courage to speak truth when it is necessary, to refuse participation in wrongdoing and to stand with those who are treated unjustly. Do not allow intimidation, pressure or the fear of consequences to turn us away from Your instructions.

Give us wisdom to distinguish courage from recklessness, and self-control to respond without hatred, cruelty or vengeance. Let our actions reflect Your justice and our words be guided by truth and compassion. Help us to protect the vulnerable, treat others fairly and remain humble before You.

Strengthen all who face threats, persecution, oppression or difficult decisions because they seek to do what is right. Protect those who speak truth at personal cost, comfort those who feel alone and give discernment to those entrusted with responsibility over others. May we encourage one another to remain steadfast rather than surrendering to fear.

Keep us faithful in the small decisions as well as the great trials. Let power be governed by love, let love be guided by truth, and let self-control preserve our conduct. May our lives demonstrate that our trust is in You, the righteous Judge who sees all things and whose justice does not fail.

In the Name of Yahusha HaMashiach, we pray.

Baruch YHVH. Emet.
