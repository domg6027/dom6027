Justice – covenantal markings rejected

🟫 MITSVOT FOCUS

The Torah establishes that YHVH's people are to be distinct in their worship, conduct and identity. Wayyiqra 19:28 prohibits making cuttings in the flesh for the dead and placing tattoo marks upon oneself. The instruction appears within a wider passage addressing practices that distinguish faithful obedience from customs associated with other forms of worship.

Devarim 14 reinforces this principle by identifying YHVH's people as His children and a qodesh people, set apart as His treasured possession. The prohibition against cutting oneself or shaving the front of the head for the dead is directly connected to this covenant identity. Their conduct was not to imitate mourning customs that disregarded the distinction between serving YHVH and following the practices of the surrounding nations.

The words “I am YHVH” remind us that these instructions are not simply cultural preferences or personal opinions. They come from the One who established the covenant and has the authority to define how His people are to live. Obedience is therefore an expression of belonging to Him.

The theme of justice also reminds us that covenant boundaries have a purpose. They protect the identity of YHVH's people from being absorbed into practices that contradict their calling. A person cannot claim covenant loyalty while deliberately disregarding the instructions that define that loyalty. Our outward conduct should be consistent with the relationship we profess.

Kĕpha 1:9–10 carries this identity into the Renewed Covenant. Those who belong to Elohim are described as a chosen race, a royal kehunnah and a qodesh nation. They are called out of darkness into His marvellous light. This identity is not something we create for ourselves; it is a calling that carries responsibility. We are to proclaim His praises through lives that reflect His character.

We must also handle this teaching with discernment and avoid judging people carelessly. The Torah specifically identifies cuttings for the dead and tattoo marks within its stated context. We should not invent additional commands, condemn people without understanding their circumstances or use outward appearance as the measure of another person's worth. The purpose of Torah is faithful obedience, not self-righteousness.

Let us therefore examine the practices we adopt, the influences we permit and the identity our conduct communicates. May we remain distinct in our covenant loyalty, refusing to imitate practices that conflict with the instructions of YHVH.

🩵 REFLECTION

Do we understand that our identity as YHVH's people carries responsibilities that must be reflected in our conduct?

Are we careful to distinguish between practices rooted in covenant obedience and customs that come from beliefs or worship contrary to the Torah?

Do we examine the origins and meaning of the practices we adopt, or do we accept them simply because they are common in the world around us?

As we prepare for the beginning of Tishi'i, let us consider what our lives communicate about the One to whom we belong. May our identity be evident through faithfulness, discernment and obedience rather than outward claims alone.

🤲🏻 PRAYER

YHVH, our Elohim, we thank You for calling us to belong to You and for giving us instructions that establish the distinction between what is qodesh and what is ordinary. Thank You for reminding us that our identity is found in our covenant relationship with You, not in the customs and expectations of the surrounding world.

Give us wisdom to understand Your Torah correctly. Help us to recognise the meaning and context of Your instructions, and protect us from adopting practices that conflict with Your ways. Teach us to examine our choices carefully and to remain faithful even when obedience differs from what is accepted around us.

Forgive us where we have followed customs without examining their origins, or where we have allowed the influence of the world to weaken our covenant loyalty. Remove pride, ignorance and self-righteousness from our hearts. Teach us to apply Your instructions with understanding, humility and compassion. Help us to live as a people who have been called out of darkness into Your marvellous light. May our conduct, our decisions and our daily lives proclaim Your praises. Give us discernment to uphold Your truth without judging others unjustly or adding burdens that You have not commanded.

As we approach the expected sighting of the renewed moon and the beginning of Tishi'i 6028 AA, guide those who watch for it. May reports be accurate, witnesses be honest and the beginning of the new month be recognised through genuine observation. Keep us attentive to Your appointed times and faithful in our walk with You.

Establish our steps in this new day. May we honour You in all that we do and remain steadfast in the covenant You have entrusted to us.

Baruch YHVH. Emet.
