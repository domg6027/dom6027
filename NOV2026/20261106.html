Shalom, Mitspacha!

⭐ THEME

Justice – honoring divine design

📘 READING

"And I commanded your judges at that time, saying, ‘When hearing between your brothers, judge righteously between a man and his brother or the stranger who is with him. Do not show partiality in right-ruling, hear the small as well as the great. Do not be afraid of anyone’s face, for the right-ruling belongs to Elohim. And the case which is too hard for you, bring it to me, and I shall hear it.’
- Devarim (Deuteronomy) 1:16–17

"My brothers, do not hold the belief of our Aḏon Yahusha ha’Mashiaḥ, the Aḏon of esteem, with partiality. For if there should come into your assembly a man with gold rings, in a splendid robe, and there should also come in a poor one dressed in rags, and you pay attention to the one wearing the splendid robe and say to him, ‘You sit here in a good place,’ and say to the poor one, ‘You stand there,’ or, ‘Sit here by my feet,’ have you not discriminated among yourselves, and become judges with wicked thoughts?
- Ya’aqoḇ (James) 2:1–4

🟫 MITSVOT FOCUS

Justice belongs to YHVH. His standards are not determined by wealth, status, appearance or human preference. He commands those entrusted with responsibility to judge righteously, without partiality, and to give equal consideration to the small and the great.

Honouring divine design means recognising that YHVH establishes the principles by which His people are to live. We are not authorised to adjust His standards to favour those we admire, those who can benefit us, or those who hold influence. Right-ruling requires integrity, impartiality and a willingness to uphold what is right even when doing so is uncomfortable.

Yahusha HaMashiach also confronted partiality among those who claimed to follow Him. Giving preferential treatment to the wealthy while humiliating the poor reveals a corrupt standard of judgment. Our conduct must reflect the justice of Elohim, showing respect and fairness to every person rather than measuring worth by outward circumstances.

🩵 REFLECTION

It is possible to speak about justice while still allowing personal preference to influence how we treat others. We may give greater attention to people with influence, overlook those who have little to offer us, or judge others according to their appearance and circumstances.

Let us examine our own conduct. Do we apply the same standard of honesty and fairness to everyone? Are we willing to defend what is right even when it does not benefit us personally?

YHVH does not show partiality, and those who belong to Him must learn to reflect His character. As we prepare for Shabbat, let us consider where our judgments, attitudes and actions need correction.

May our lives demonstrate that we honour the justice of YHVH, respect His design and treat others with the dignity and fairness He requires.

🤲🏻 PRAYER

YHVH, righteous Elohim, thank You that Your judgments are true and that Your ways are without partiality. Teach us to recognise Your standards and to honour the order and design You have established.

Forgive us where personal preference, pride, prejudice or outward appearances have influenced our judgments. Remove every desire to favour the wealthy, influential or familiar while neglecting those who are poor, vulnerable or overlooked.

Give us courage to uphold what is right, wisdom to make sound decisions and humility to recognise when our own thinking needs correction. Help us to treat every person with fairness, honesty and respect, without compromising Your Word.

As we enter Shabbat, prepare our hearts to rest in Your truth. Let our homes and gatherings reflect Your justice, integrity and peace. May our conduct bring esteem to Your Name.

Baruch YHVH. Emet.
