Shalom, Mitspacha!

THEME

Prohibition of mourning practices tied to idolatry

READING

“You are the children of YHVH your Elohim. Do not cut yourselves nor shave the front of your head for the dead. For you are a set-apart people to YHVH your Elohim, and YHVH has chosen you to be a people for Himself, a treasured possession above all the peoples who are on the face of the earth.”
- Devarim (Deuteronomy) 14:1–2

“Do not make any cuttings in your flesh for the dead, nor put tattoo marks on yourselves. I am YHVH.”
- Wayyiqra (Leviticus) 19:28

“But, brothers, we do not wish you to be ignorant concerning those who have fallen asleep, lest you be sad as others who have no expectation. For if we believe that Yahusha died and rose again, so also Elohim shall bring with Him those who sleep in Yahusha.”
- Tas'loniqim Aleph (1 Thessalonians) 4:13–14

MITSVOT FOCUS

YHVH established a clear distinction between His set-apart people and the practices of the nations around them. Devarim 14 reminds Yisra’el that our identity belongs to YHVH. We are not to imitate mourning customs rooted in idolatry, nor are we to use our bodies in rituals that contradict the holiness to which He has called us.

The prohibition is not a command to suppress sorrow or pretend that death does not hurt. Grief is a genuine human response to loss. The Torah does not forbid tears, remembrance, or the expression of sorrow. It forbids adopting practices that belong to false worship and treating death through rituals that dishonour the Creator.

Our bodies are not ours to mark or injure in imitation of pagan customs. They are part of the life entrusted to us by YHVH. Our conduct, including the way we respond to death, must reflect our covenant identity. We must not allow the customs of the surrounding world to determine how we honour the dead or express our grief.

The Renewed Covenant gives us an additional foundation for this understanding. Sha’ul does not tell believers that they must never grieve. He teaches that our grief must not be without expectation. Yahusha HaMashiach died and rose again, and this promise changes how we face death. We may mourn, but we do not mourn as those who have no hope.

Therefore, we must distinguish between remembering someone with love and participating in rituals that assign spiritual power to the dead, seek their intervention, or imitate idolatrous customs. Our comfort and expectation must remain in YHVH, not in practices that He has forbidden.

Obedience in this matter is not merely about avoiding a particular outward action. It is about remembering who we are, whose people we belong to, and where our hope is placed. Our mourning, our remembrance, and our conduct must remain consistent with our covenant relationship with YHVH.

REFLECTION

When we experience the death of someone we love, do we allow grief to remain within the boundaries established by YHVH, or do we sometimes accept customs simply because they are familiar or culturally expected?

Can we distinguish between honouring the memory of the departed and participating in practices that give the dead a spiritual role that belongs to YHVH alone?

Our sorrow does not make us disobedient, and our tears do not demonstrate a lack of faith. What matters is that we do not surrender our covenant identity when faced with loss. We can grieve honestly, remember lovingly, and still hold firmly to the expectation given through Yahusha HaMashiach.

Let our lives demonstrate that even in sorrow, we belong to YHVH. May our hope remain anchored in His promises rather than in the customs and fears of the world.

PRAYER

YHVH our Elohim, we thank You that You have called us to be Your set-apart people and have given us instructions to distinguish our conduct from the practices of the nations.

Teach us to honour Your commandments even when we face grief, loss and difficult circumstances. Give us discernment to recognise customs that are rooted in idolatry, and the courage to refuse practices that do not honour You.

Comfort those who are mourning. Strengthen those who have lost loved ones, and help them to grieve without losing sight of the expectation You have given us through Yahusha HaMashiach. Remind us that our hope is not found in the dead, in rituals, or in the traditions of men, but in You and in Your promise of resurrection.

Help us to honour the lives of those who have passed without crossing the boundaries You have established. May our words, actions and remembrance reflect reverence for You and faithfulness to Your covenant.

Keep our hearts steadfast, our minds discerning and our conduct set apart. May we remain faithful in every season of life, including the seasons of sorrow.

In the Name of Yahusha HaMashiach, we pray.

Baruch YHVH. Emet.
