⭐ THEME

Justice - Mercy Within Obligation

🟫 MITSVOT FOCUS

YHVH's Torah establishes obligations that protect justice while making room for mercy. The landowner was entitled to harvest from his field, olive trees and vineyard, yet he was not permitted to gather everything for himself. What remained was deliberately made available to the stranger, the fatherless and the widow.

This was not merely an optional act of generosity. It was part of the righteous ordering of community life. YHVH placed an obligation upon those who possessed resources to leave provision for those who were vulnerable.

The command also connected present responsibility with remembered history. Yisrael was to remember its own experience as a vulnerable people in Mitsrayim. Remembering what YHVH had done for them was meant to shape how they treated others.

🩵 REFLECTION

Justice is not only about enforcing what is right; it is also about recognising when our rights and responsibilities affect people who have less power or fewer resources.

YHVH does not call us to ignore obligation in the name of mercy, nor to practise justice without compassion. His Torah brings the two together. We are responsible for what belongs to us, but we are also responsible for how our abundance affects others.

May we therefore examine whether we hold too tightly to what we have been given. May our remembrance of YHVH's mercy toward us make us more willing to show mercy toward others. What we possess is not merely an opportunity for personal benefit; it can also become a means through which YHVH provides for someone else.

👐 PRAYER

Heavenly Father, YHVH, thank You for teaching us justice, mercy and responsible stewardship. Help us to recognise the needs of those around us and to use what You have entrusted to us in ways that honour You. Keep us from selfishness, greed and indifference. Give us compassionate hearts that remember Your mercy and willingly make room for others. Teach us to fulfil our obligations faithfully while showing kindness to those who are vulnerable. May our lives reflect Your righteousness and generosity in everything we do.

Baruch YHVH. Emet.
