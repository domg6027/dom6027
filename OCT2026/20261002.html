⭐ THEME

Wisdom for life

🤲🏻 OPENING PRAYER

YHVH our Elohim, we come before You on this Erev Shabbat with grateful hearts. Thank You for the gift of life, for the wisdom You give through Your Torah, and for the seasons and circumstances through which You teach us to walk faithfully before You.

Teach us to seek wisdom that begins with reverence for You rather than with confidence in our own understanding. Help us to recognise that knowledge alone does not make a person wise. Give us wisdom to discern what is right, when to act, when to wait, when to speak, when to remain silent, and how to respond faithfully to the circumstances You allow into our lives.

Help us to understand that wisdom is demonstrated through obedience. Keep us from separating what we believe from how we live. May our choices, relationships, responsibilities, words and priorities reflect the understanding You are teaching us.

As we come toward the completion of this journey through Sukkot, remind us that every season has a purpose. Teach us to receive what each season has taught us, to release what must remain behind, and to carry forward what will strengthen our walk with You.

Give us humble hearts that are willing to learn, correctable spirits that are willing to change, and faithful hearts that continue walking with You when the way ahead is not yet fully visible.

Baruch YHVH. Emet.

🍷 COMMUNION

As we partake of the bread and the fruit of the vine, we remember Yahusha HaMashiach and the covenant represented through His body and blood.

Covenant life requires more than receiving knowledge. We are called to live according to what YHVH has revealed. Wisdom is therefore not merely the ability to understand words, but the willingness to allow truth to shape our conduct.

May this remembrance cause us to examine how we use what we have learned. Knowledge can increase pride when it is separated from obedience, but wisdom teaches us to walk humbly before YHVH.

Teach us to value correction rather than resist it. When Your Torah exposes an error in our thinking or conduct, give us humility to receive correction. When circumstances reveal weaknesses in our character, help us to learn rather than merely complain. When we experience blessing, teach us gratitude. When we experience difficulty, teach us perseverance.

May our lives demonstrate that the wisdom You give is producing fruit. Let our speech become more careful, our decisions more thoughtful, our relationships more faithful, and our obedience more sincere.

As we share this remembrance, keep us mindful that Yahusha HaMashiach called His followers to walk in the ways of YHVH. May our covenant life therefore be marked by wisdom expressed through faithful action.

📖 MESSAGE

Wisdom for life begins with recognising that life belongs to YHVH. We do not create the order of life; we discover it and learn to live faithfully within it.

Human beings often want certainty before they act. We want to understand the entire path before taking the next step. Yet wisdom does not require us to know everything. Wisdom teaches us to trust YHVH sufficiently to obey what He has already revealed.

This is why the fear of YHVH is the beginning of wisdom. Reverence establishes the correct relationship between the Creator and the creature. We are not the ultimate authority over our lives. We are accountable to the One who gave us life.

Wisdom also teaches us the difference between knowledge and understanding. A person may possess considerable knowledge and still make foolish decisions. Information can tell us what exists, but wisdom helps us understand how we should respond. Torah gives us knowledge of YHVH's ways, while obedience demonstrates whether that knowledge has become wisdom in our lives.

There are therefore times when wisdom requires action and times when it requires restraint. There are moments to speak and moments to remain silent. There are circumstances in which we must move forward and others in which we must wait. Wisdom does not treat every situation identically because wisdom recognises the season.

This does not mean that truth changes with circumstances. YHVH's Torah remains the foundation. What changes is the situation in which we must apply what we have learned. Wisdom enables us to live faithfully within changing circumstances without changing the standard of obedience.

Wisdom also requires humility. None of us sees the whole picture. Our understanding is limited, our perspective is partial, and our emotions can distort our judgment. This is why we are warned not to lean upon our own understanding. Trusting YHVH does not mean abandoning thought or discernment. It means recognising that our understanding must remain submitted to Him.

Correction is therefore part of wisdom. A wise person is not someone who never needs correction. A wise person is willing to receive correction and learn from it. When YHVH exposes something in our lives that needs to change, wisdom does not defend the error merely because it has become familiar.

The same principle applies to the seasons of life. Some seasons are productive and joyful. Others are difficult, uncertain or quiet. Some seasons bring increase, while others require endurance and restraint. Wisdom does not measure YHVH's faithfulness only by whether circumstances are comfortable. It learns to recognise His purposes even when the season is difficult.

As we approach the end of this Sukkot journey, we can look back and ask what we have learned. Have we become more dependent upon YHVH? Have we become more grateful for His provision? Have we learned to recognise the temporary nature of earthly security? Have we allowed His Torah to correct our priorities?

The purpose of a journey is not merely to arrive at the destination. The journey changes the traveller. What we experience, learn, surrender and practise along the way becomes part of who we are when we reach the next stage.

Wisdom for life therefore means carrying the lessons of one season into the next. We do not need to repeat every mistake in order to learn from it. We do not need to remain in every place we have occupied in the past. We can receive correction, give thanks for what YHVH has taught us, and continue forward with greater understanding.

May our wisdom never become merely words. May it become visible in the way we live.

🌙 SUKKOT DAY 6: Completion of the Journey

Sukkot Day 6 brings us close to the completion of the appointed journey. The sukkah has continually reminded us that earthly dwelling is temporary, while YHVH remains our enduring refuge.

A journey has a beginning, a path and a destination. As we approach the final day of Sukkot, we can reflect upon what this appointed time has taught us about dwelling, dependence, joy, provision, light and living water.

The completion of a journey does not mean that everything has ended. It means that one stage has reached its appointed conclusion and prepares us for what comes next. YHVH leads His people through seasons and stages, teaching us through each one.

May the completion of this Sukkot journey remind us that we are not wandering without purpose. YHVH is faithful in every stage. When the path is clear, He is faithful. When the way is difficult, He is faithful. When one season ends and another begins, He remains faithful.

May we carry the lessons of Sukkot beyond the sukkah. Let our dependence upon YHVH continue when the appointed days are complete. Let our gratitude continue when the gathering ends. Let our obedience continue when the special season becomes ordinary life again.

The temporary dwelling points beyond itself. The journey points beyond itself. The appointed time points us toward faithful living with YHVH.

As we approach the completion of Sukkot, may we therefore finish this stage with gratitude and begin the next with renewed faithfulness.

🤲🏻 CLOSING PRAYER

YHVH our Elohim, thank You for the wisdom You give us through Your Torah and for every lesson You have placed along the journey of our lives.

Teach us to fear You, trust You and obey You. Keep us from leaning upon our own understanding when Your ways challenge our assumptions. Give us humility to receive correction and courage to put what we have learned into practice.

Thank You for bringing us through another stage of this appointed journey. As Sukkot draws toward completion, help us to recognise what You have taught us about dependence, provision, gratitude, temporary earthly dwelling and Your enduring presence.

Where we have learned, help us remember. Where we have failed, help us repent. Where we have grown, help us remain humble. Where we still lack understanding, teach us patiently.

Prepare us for the next season You have appointed. May we not merely remember these holy days, but carry their lessons into our daily lives.

Let our homes, relationships, work, decisions and worship reflect the wisdom You have given us. May we walk carefully, speak truthfully, act justly and remain faithful to Your Torah.

As this journey approaches completion, keep our eyes upon You. May we finish this stage with gratitude and continue forward with trust.

Baruch YHVH. Emet.
