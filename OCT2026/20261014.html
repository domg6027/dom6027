⭐ THEME

Testing words against Torah

📘 READING

“If a prophet or a dreamer of dreams arises among you and gives you a sign or a wonder, and the sign or wonder takes place, of which he spoke to you, saying, ‘Let us go after other elohim’ — which you have not known — ‘and let us serve them,’ you shall not listen to the words of that prophet or that dreamer of dreams, for YHVH your Elohim is testing you to know whether you love YHVH your Elohim with all your heart and with all your being. You shall walk after YHVH your Elohim, and fear Him, and guard His commands, and obey His voice, and serve Him, and cling to Him.”
- Devarim (Deuteronomy) 13:1–4

“To the Torah and to the witness! If they do not speak according to this word, it is because there is no light in them.”
- Yeshayahu (Isaiah) 8:20

“For the command which I am commanding you today is not too difficult for you, nor is it far off. It is not in the heavens, for you to say, ‘Who shall go up for us to the heavens and bring it to us, and let us hear it, so that we may do it?’ Nor is it beyond the sea, for you to say, ‘Who shall cross over the sea for us and bring it to us, and let us hear it, so that we may do it?’ For the word is very near you, in your mouth and in your heart, to do it.”
- Devarim (Deuteronomy) 30:11–14

🟫 MITSVOT FOCUS

YHVH does not ask us to accept every spiritual claim simply because it sounds convincing or is accompanied by an extraordinary sign. Devarim warns us that even an impressive sign cannot justify leading people away from YHVH and His commands.

The standard for testing spiritual teaching is not a speaker’s popularity, confidence, reputation, or claimed authority. We must examine whether the message agrees with Torah and remains faithful to the Scriptures. A claim that contradicts YHVH’s instructions does not become true because the person presenting it appears sincere or powerful.

Yeshayahu directs us back to the Torah and the witness. We must therefore distinguish between what Scripture actually says and what a teacher, interpreter, or spiritual messenger claims it means. Personal impressions and interpretations must never be placed above YHVH’s established instruction.

Devarim also reminds us that YHVH’s instruction is not beyond our reach. We are responsible for learning it, applying it, and allowing it to guide our choices. Testing a message is not an excuse for endless suspicion; it is an act of faithful obedience.

🩵 REFLECTION

When we hear a teaching that is new, exciting, frightening, or comforting, do we test it against Torah before accepting it? Or do we allow the confidence of the speaker or the strength of our own emotions to decide what we believe?

We should read Scripture in context, compare related passages, and be willing to correct our understanding when the evidence requires it. We must not select isolated verses to defend a conclusion we have already decided to believe.

Testing words against Torah also requires humility. We may misunderstand a passage, repeat an error, or hold an interpretation that needs correction. A teachable heart does not fear the truth; it welcomes correction and returns to YHVH’s instruction.

Today, let us be careful listeners and responsible speakers. May our beliefs, teaching, and conduct be shaped by the Word of YHVH rather than by human persuasion.

👐 PRAYER

YHVH, teach us to test every teaching against Your Torah and to recognise when words lead away from Your instruction. Give us wisdom to understand Scripture in context, humility to correct our errors, and courage to remain faithful to Your truth.

Guard us from deception and help us walk in obedience with sincere hearts.

Baruch YHVH. Emet.
