🤲🏻 OPENING PRAYER

Heavenly Father, YHVH our Elohim, we come before You on this Shabbat with grateful hearts. Thank You for bringing us through another week and granting us this set-apart time to rest, reflect, learn and draw nearer to You. Thank You for Your Torah, which reveals Your ways and teaches us to distinguish between what is righteous and what dishonours Your Name.

As we reflect on the prohibition of using Your Name in vain, examine our hearts and our speech. Teach us to understand that Your Name is not a casual expression, a tool for gaining influence, or a phrase to be used carelessly. Your Name represents Your identity, authority, character and reputation. Help us to speak of You with reverence and to ensure that our conduct does not contradict the truth we profess.

As we enter Week 43, with its theme of calling to return, awaken us to the places where we have drifted from Your ways. Through the words of Isaiah, remind us that You call Your people away from empty religion, injustice and disobedience, and back to a life shaped by righteousness, mercy and faithfulness.

Give us ears to hear Your correction without becoming defensive. Give us hearts that are willing to repent, hands ready to do what is right, and tongues that speak truthfully. May this Shabbat be a time of genuine renewal, not merely a pause in our routine.

Prepare us to remember Yahusha HaMashiach and the covenant represented through His body and blood. Let everything we do today be governed by reverence for You and a sincere desire to walk in Your truth.

Baruch YHVH. Emet.

🍷 COMMUNION

We remember Yahusha HaMashiach and the covenant represented through His body and blood. As we partake of the bread and the cup, let us remember that covenant faithfulness involves our whole lives. The words we speak, the promises we make, the way we treat others and the character we display all bear witness to the Elohim whom we profess to serve.

To use the Name of YHVH in vain is not limited to speaking it irreverently. We also dishonour His Name when we invoke His authority to support falsehood, manipulate others, excuse wrongdoing or make claims that do not reflect His truth. To speak in His Name carries responsibility.

The covenant calls us to integrity. If we confess that YHVH is our Elohim, our lives should increasingly reflect His righteousness. We cannot separate our profession of faith from our conduct and then expect our words alone to establish our faithfulness.

As we remember Yahusha HaMashiach, let us examine whether our speech and actions agree with the covenant we profess. Where our words have been careless, teach us to speak with greater reverence. Where we have used spiritual language to defend our own desires, humble us. Where our conduct has misrepresented Your character, lead us to repentance and correction.

May this remembrance strengthen our commitment to honour Your Name, to live truthfully before You and to treat others with the justice and mercy Your Torah requires.

📖 MESSAGE

The prohibition against using the Name of YHVH in vain calls us to recognise the holiness of the One whose Name we bear upon our lips. His Name is not an ordinary word to be used without thought. It identifies the Elohim who created us, established His covenant, gave His Torah and calls His people to walk in righteousness.

The commandment is often reduced to the question of whether someone has spoken the Name carelessly or used it as an expression of frustration. Such speech deserves serious consideration, but the command reaches further than casual or irreverent language. The Torah also warns against invoking the Name falsely and thereby profaning it. The issue includes the truthfulness, integrity and reverence with which we use His Name.

When a person invokes YHVH to support a false statement, a dishonest promise or an unjust action, the Name is being used to give an appearance of authority to something that does not reflect His character. This is a serious matter because it misrepresents the One who is righteous and true.

Our speech can therefore either honour the Name or bring dishonour to it. When we speak truthfully, keep our commitments and act with justice, our conduct can reflect the character of the Elohim we profess to serve. When we deceive, manipulate, oppress or act hypocritically while claiming His authority, our conduct contradicts our confession.

This is why reverence for the Name cannot be separated from obedience. YHVH does not merely call us to pronounce His Name correctly or to avoid certain expressions. He calls us to recognise His authority and to live in a manner consistent with what He has revealed. Reverence begins in the heart, becomes evident in our speech and must be demonstrated through our actions.

The warning against false oaths in Vayiqra reinforces this principle. A person must not use the Name as a guarantee for something untrue. Neither should we make promises lightly, claiming certainty or divine backing when we have no right to do so. Words carry responsibility, particularly when we attach the authority of YHVH to them.

We must also be careful not to claim that YHVH has spoken when He has not given us a word. Spiritual language can influence people deeply. When someone declares a personal opinion to be a command from YHVH, that person risks placing human authority where only His authority belongs. Humility requires us to distinguish between what Scripture plainly teaches, what we understand from it and what remains our own interpretation.

The same principle applies to the way we represent YHVH to others. We cannot claim to uphold His Name while deliberately treating people unjustly. We cannot speak constantly about holiness while refusing correction. We cannot use the language of truth to conceal selfish motives. Such contradictions damage our witness because our actions give people a distorted picture of the Elohim we claim to honour.

This brings us to the weekly theme, Calling to return. Through Yesha'yahu, YHVH confronts a people who may continue religious activities while their conduct contradicts His righteousness. He calls them to wash themselves, cease doing evil, learn to do good, seek justice and defend those who are vulnerable. Their return must be visible in changed behaviour, not merely in religious words.

The call to return is therefore directly connected to the honour due to the Name. Repentance means more than expressing regret or repeating words of confession. It requires us to turn away from what is wrong and begin walking in what is right. If our speech has dishonoured YHVH, we must learn to speak truthfully. If we have misused His authority, we must stop making claims that exceed what He has revealed. If our actions have harmed others while we claimed to represent Him, we must acknowledge the wrongdoing and seek to correct what can be corrected.

YHVH's call through Isaiah also reminds us that genuine devotion cannot be separated from justice. A person may speak reverently, observe appointed times and participate in worship, yet still need to repent of dishonesty, cruelty, exploitation or indifference to those in need. Religious activity cannot serve as a substitute for obedience. The One whose Name we honour is also the One who commands us to do good and seek justice.

This does not mean that every mistake makes a person a deliberate profaner of the Name. We must distinguish between an error that requires correction and a wilful misuse of divine authority. The proper response to our shortcomings is neither careless indifference nor despair. It is humility, honest examination, repentance and a renewed commitment to walk according to Torah.

The call to return also offers hope. Yesha'yahu declares that the unrighteous person should forsake his way and return to YHVH, who is merciful and ready to pardon. Correction is not given merely to condemn us; it exposes the path away from wrongdoing and calls us back to faithfulness. We should not resist that correction because it challenges our habits, traditions or self-image.

As we observe Shabbat, let us consider how we use the Name of YHVH in our conversations, prayers, promises and public declarations. Do our words accurately reflect what we know from His Word? Do we invoke His authority to strengthen our own opinions? Do we speak of His justice while overlooking injustice in our own conduct? Are we willing to return when His Torah reveals that we have gone astray?

The Name of YHVH is to be honoured in truth. Our responsibility is not merely to speak of Him, but to represent Him faithfully through reverence, honesty, justice and obedience. May our words never become a cover for disobedience, and may our worship never become a substitute for a transformed life.

The call to return remains before us: forsake what is wrong, learn to do good, seek justice and walk faithfully in the ways YHVH has revealed. In doing so, we honour His Name not only with our lips, but with the whole of our lives.

🙏🏻 CLOSING PRAYER

YHVH our Elohim, we thank You for Your Torah and for the correction You give us through Your Word. Thank You for reminding us that Your Name is holy and must never be used to support falsehood, manipulation or unrighteousness. Forgive us for every careless word, every false claim and every occasion when our conduct has misrepresented Your character. Search our hearts and expose the places where pride, fear, convenience or personal ambition has influenced our speech. Teach us to recognise the weight of our words and to speak with truthfulness, humility and reverence.

As we reflect on Your call to return, give us the courage to forsake what is wrong and the willingness to learn what is good. Where we have caused harm, show us how to take responsibility. Where we have been unjust, lead us to act righteously. Where we have resisted correction, soften our hearts so that we may receive Your instruction. Teach us to honour Your Name in our homes, our conversations, our worship and our dealings with others. May our lives bear truthful witness to who You are. Keep us faithful to Your Torah, and guide us in the way of righteousness.

We pray that You strengthen families to build relationships founded upon honesty, faithfulness, respect and responsible conduct. Protect marriages from betrayal and households from deception, manipulation and division.

We pray that You give parents wisdom to teach their children the value of truth and loyalty. Help children to grow in discernment, integrity and respect for the responsibilities entrusted to them.

We pray that You protect Your people among the nations. Give wisdom to those who hold positions of authority, and restrain those who seek to use power for oppression, deception or destruction.

We pray that You strengthen those who must make difficult decisions for the protection of others. Give them the courage of Esther, the loyalty of Mordekai and the wisdom to act at the right time.

We pray for peace in the Middle East and for the protection of innocent people affected by war and violence. Strengthen and comfort the elderly, protect farmers and those whose livelihoods depend upon the land, and watch over people facing war, disaster and displacement.

We pray that You strengthen and protect evangelists, teachers and missionaries who faithfully proclaim Your Word. Give them wisdom, protection and endurance.

We pray that You bring healing and comfort to those who are ill, suffering or afraid. Prepare Your people for the coming tribulation and teach us to remain faithful under pressure.

We pray that You bless and strengthen Derech Olam Mishkan International. Help us to serve faithfully, preserve truth, encourage Your people and remain steadfast in Your covenant.

Bless this Shabbat with rest, understanding and renewed commitment. May we leave this set-apart time more determined to honour You in both word and deed.

Baruch YHVH. Emet.
