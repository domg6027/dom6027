⭐ THEME

Reverence in speech / Justice – sanctifying the tongue

📘 READING

“You shall not take the Name of YHVH your Elohim in vain, for YHVH will not hold him guiltless who takes His Name in vain.”
- Shemot (Exodus) 20:7

“You shall not go about as a slanderer among your people, nor shall you stand against the blood of your neighbour. I am YHVH.”
- Vayiqra (Leviticus) 19:16

“Even so the tongue is a little member and boasts great things. See how great a forest a little fire kindles! And the tongue is a fire, the world of unrighteousness. The tongue is so set among our members that it defiles the entire body, and sets on fire the course of life, and is set on fire by Gehenna.”
- Ya'aqob (James) 3:5–6

🟫 MITSVOT FOCUS

YHVH calls us to treat speech as a matter of righteousness, not merely personal preference. Our words reveal what we honour, what we believe, and how seriously we take our responsibility toward Him and our fellow human beings. Reverence in speech begins with recognising that the Name of YHVH is set apart and must never be treated as an empty expression, a careless exclamation, or a means of giving false authority to our own opinions.

However, reverence extends beyond the way we pronounce or speak about His Name. We cannot claim to honour YHVH with our mouths while using the same mouths to deceive, slander, humiliate, threaten, manipulate or destroy the reputations of others. A tongue that speaks religious words but delights in falsehood does not demonstrate the reverence it professes.

The command against bearing false witness and the prohibition against slander show that speech has consequences for justice. Words can influence how others are judged, whether someone is trusted, how a dispute develops, and whether an innocent person suffers. A careless accusation may travel farther than the truth that eventually corrects it. A rumour may damage a person's reputation long after its source has been forgotten.

Justice therefore requires more than refusing to tell an obvious lie. It calls us to examine what we repeat, how we describe another person, and whether we have sufficient evidence before making a claim. Something does not become true simply because many people repeat it. Nor does a statement become righteous merely because the speaker believes the cause is important.

Sanctifying the tongue means placing our speech under the authority of Torah. Before speaking, we should ask whether our words are truthful, whether they are fair, whether they are necessary, and whether they respect the dignity and rights of those concerned. When correction is needed, it should serve righteousness rather than revenge. When wrongdoing must be exposed, it should be addressed honestly and responsibly rather than through exaggeration, malicious gossip or public humiliation for its own sake.

There is also a difference between silence that protects injustice and restraint that prevents injustice. We must not use the demand for polite speech as an excuse to conceal wrongdoing or abandon those who need protection. At the same time, we must not confuse anger with courage or harshness with truth. Righteous speech may be firm, direct and uncompromising without becoming deceitful, abusive or needlessly destructive.

The tongue is small, but its influence can be enormous. It can encourage someone who has lost hope, restore understanding between people, expose deception, defend the vulnerable and communicate truth. It can also deepen division, spread suspicion and turn disagreement into hostility. The question is not merely whether we possess the freedom to speak, but whether we exercise that freedom in a way that honours YHVH and serves justice.

🩵 REFLECTION

Reverence in speech and justice in speech belong together because the way we speak about YHVH must agree with the way we treat the people He has created. We cannot separate devotion from conduct, nor can we claim that our words are righteous simply because they contain sacred expressions. True reverence shapes our character, and a character submitted to YHVH learns to handle words with care.

We should examine the ordinary conversations in which our tongues are most likely to become careless. Do we repeat information before verifying it? Do we present assumptions as facts? Do we reveal private matters simply because we know them? Do we use sarcasm to wound others while pretending that we are only joking? Do we speak differently about someone when that person is absent than we would speak when standing face to face with them?

We should also examine how we respond when we are challenged. Pride can make correction feel like an attack, and anger can tempt us to answer before we have understood what was said. A sanctified tongue does not require us to agree with every person or remain silent in the face of falsehood. It requires us to distinguish between defending truth and defending our own pride.

When we discover that we have spoken wrongly, we must be willing to acknowledge the fault and correct the harm as far as possible. An apology does not erase every consequence, but refusing to admit wrongdoing allows the damage to continue. Where we have spread false information, we should correct it with the same seriousness with which we spread it. Where we have harmed someone's reputation, repentance must move us toward responsible action rather than empty regret.

The same standard applies when we hear others speaking. We need discernment to recognise the difference between truthful reporting, necessary correction, malicious gossip and unsupported accusation. We should not become participants in injustice merely because a conversation is popular, entertaining or emotionally satisfying. Sometimes the most righteous response is to ask for evidence, refuse to repeat a rumour, defend someone who is being treated unfairly, or redirect the conversation toward truth.

Let us remember that sanctifying the tongue is not a once-off achievement. It is a daily discipline that requires humility, self-control and a willingness to be corrected. Our words should increasingly reflect the character of the Elohim we profess to serve. May our speech honour His Name, uphold truth, protect the innocent and contribute to justice rather than confusion.

👐 PRAYER

Heavenly Father, YHVH our Elohim, we thank You for Your Torah, which teaches us to distinguish between righteousness and wrongdoing. Thank You for reminding us that our speech is not insignificant and that the words we choose can either serve truth or contribute to injustice.

Examine our hearts and reveal where our tongues have been careless, proud, dishonest or unkind. Forgive us for every time we have spoken falsely, repeated an unverified claim, damaged another person's reputation, used words to manipulate others, or invoked Your Name without the reverence it deserves. Give us the humility to acknowledge our failures and the courage to make matters right wherever we can.

Teach us to pause before speaking and to weigh our words according to Your truth. Help us to distinguish facts from assumptions, correction from condemnation, and righteous firmness from uncontrolled anger. Keep us from slander, malicious gossip, exaggeration and false testimony. May we never use sacred language to excuse conduct that contradicts Your instructions.

Strengthen those who are being harmed by false accusations, malicious rumours or unjust judgment. Protect the vulnerable and give wisdom to those who must investigate wrongdoing, make difficult decisions or speak on behalf of those who cannot adequately defend themselves. Let truth come to light, and restrain those who would use words to deceive, intimidate or destroy.

Help us to use our tongues for what is good: to encourage the discouraged, comfort the suffering, teach truth, correct wrongdoing responsibly, reconcile where reconciliation is righteous, and defend those who are treated unfairly. When we must speak difficult truths, grant us courage and discernment. When silence is wiser, teach us restraint. When we are corrected, give us humble hearts that are willing to learn.

May our families and households be strengthened through honesty, faithfulness, respect and responsible conduct. Protect our marriages from betrayal and our homes from deception, manipulation and division. Give parents wisdom to teach their children truth and loyalty, and help children grow in discernment, integrity and respect for the responsibilities entrusted to them.

Protect Your people among the nations. Give wisdom to those in authority, and restrain those who use power for oppression, deception or destruction. Strengthen those who must make difficult decisions to protect others, giving them the courage of Esther, the loyalty of Mordekai and the wisdom to act at the right time.

We pray for peace in the Middle East and protection for innocent people affected by war and violence. Strengthen and comfort the elderly, protect farmers and those whose livelihoods depend upon the land, and watch over all who face war, disaster and displacement.

Strengthen and protect evangelists, teachers and missionaries who faithfully proclaim Your Word. Give them wisdom, protection and endurance, and guard their speech so that they communicate Your truth with integrity.

Bring healing and comfort to those who are ill, suffering or afraid. Prepare Your people for the coming tribulation and teach us to remain faithful under pressure, refusing to surrender truth or righteousness to fear.

Bless and strengthen Derech Olam Mishkan International. Help us serve faithfully, preserve truth, encourage Your people and remain steadfast in Your covenant. Let our words and conduct bear a consistent witness to the truth we profess.

May the words of our mouths and the meditation of our hearts be acceptable before You, YHVH, our Rock and our Redeemer. Let reverence govern our speech and justice guide the use of our tongues, so that we honour Your Name not only with our words but also through the righteousness of our conduct.

Baruch YHVH. Emet.
