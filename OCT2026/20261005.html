⭐ THEME

Rejecting physical representations

📘 READING

“Therefore watch yourselves carefully, for you saw no form when YHVH spoke to you at Horev from the midst of the fire.”
- Devarim (Deuteronomy) 4:15

“You shall not make for yourselves an idol, nor any image of anything that is in heaven above, or that is on the earth beneath, or that is in the water under the earth.”
- Shemot (Exodus) 20:4

“Guard yourselves, lest you forget the covenant of YHVH your Elohim which He made with you, and make for yourselves a carved image in the form of anything which YHVH your Elohim has forbidden you.”
- Devarim (Deuteronomy) 4:23

🟫 MITSVOT FOCUS

YHVH does not give us permission to reduce Him to a physical representation. When Yisra'el heard His voice at Horev, they did not see a form. This distinction matters because YHVH calls us to know Him through His revealed Word, His actions and His commandments rather than through an object made by human hands.

The prohibition against physical representations is not limited to recognising that an image is false. It also guards the heart against the tendency to make the invisible Elohim manageable, controllable or familiar through something we can see and touch.

We must therefore examine not only obvious idols, but also the subtle ways in which human imagination can create representations of Elohim that He has not authorised. Religious objects, pictures, symbols and images can become substitutes for faithful obedience when people begin to attach to them a holiness, authority or presence that belongs to YHVH alone.

The mitsvah calls us back to reverence. We do not need to manufacture a visible likeness of YHVH in order to draw near to Him. We draw near through obedience, prayer, His Torah and the truth He has revealed.

🩵 REFLECTION

Rejecting physical representations requires more than removing an image from our surroundings. It requires us to examine the images we may have created in our own minds.

It is easy to reshape Elohim according to what feels comfortable to us. We may imagine Him as less demanding than His Torah declares, more tolerant of disobedience than His Word permits, or more like human authority than the Creator and Judge He has revealed Himself to be.

The answer is not to invent a better image. The answer is to return to what YHVH has actually spoken.

After the Mo'edim marathon of 6028 AA, we now return to ordinary days. This makes the lesson especially appropriate. The faithfulness we have practised during appointed days must continue when there is no visible festival surrounding us. We must not depend upon physical reminders to make YHVH real to us. His truth remains real because He has spoken.

Let us therefore reject every representation that diminishes His holiness, distorts His character or replaces His revealed Word. Let us know Him according to what He has made known, and walk faithfully in what He has commanded.

👐 PRAYER

YHVH our Elohim, keep our hearts from creating representations of You that You have not given us.

Teach us to recognise the difference between what You have revealed and what human imagination has invented. Keep us from giving created things the honour, authority or holiness that belongs to You alone.

Guard us against every visible or invisible substitute that could take Your place in our hearts. Help us to test what we believe against Your Word rather than reshaping Your Word according to what is comfortable to us.

As we return to the ordinary days following the Mo'edim of 6028 AA, help us to remain faithful when the appointed days have passed. Let our obedience continue without needing outward signs to sustain it.

Keep us attentive to Your voice, faithful to Your Torah and humble before You. May we seek to know You as You have revealed Yourself rather than according to the images we might create.

Baruch YHVH. Emet.
