📨 DAVAR LECHEM – Yom Shlishi, Shmini 2, 6028 AA

Shalom, Mitspacha!

⭐ THEME

Prohibition of false prophecy

📘 READING

“But the prophet who presumes to speak a word in My Name, which I have not commanded him to speak, or who speaks in the name of other elohim, that prophet shall die. And if you say in your heart, ‘How shall we know the word which YHVH has not spoken?’ — when a prophet speaks in the Name of YHVH, if the word does not take place or come to pass, that is the word which YHVH has not spoken. The prophet has spoken it presumptuously; do not be afraid of him.”
- Devarim (Deuteronomy) 18:20–22

“Do not listen to the words of the prophets who prophesy to you. They make you worthless; they speak a vision of their own heart, not from the mouth of YHVH. They continually say to those who despise Me, ‘YHVH has said, “You shall have peace”’; and to everyone who walks according to the stubbornness of his own heart, they say, ‘No evil shall come upon you.’”
- Yirmeyahu (Jeremiah) 23:16–17

“Beloved ones, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits, whether they are of Elohim; because many false prophets have gone out into the world.”
- 1 Yochanan (1 John) 4:1

🟫 MITSVOT FOCUS

YHVH takes truth seriously. Prophecy is not a platform for personal ambition, nor may anyone present a personal opinion as divine authority.

Devarim warns against presumptuous prophecy. Our thoughts, predictions, desires, and interpretations must never be presented as revelations from YHVH when He has not spoken. Sincerity, emotion, popularity, and impressive speech do not replace truth.

Yirmeyahu warns against telling people only what they want to hear or offering false reassurance. A message that comforts us is not necessarily a message from YHVH.

We must test spiritual claims against Torah and the whole counsel of Scripture. We must distinguish what Scripture actually says from our interpretations and claims of personal revelation.

Those who speak prophetically must do so responsibly. We must not manipulate people through fear, promise what YHVH has not promised, or present uncertain interpretations as unquestionable truth. When a claim proves false, we must acknowledge it rather than excuse it.

🩵 REFLECTION

Do we accept a message because it sounds powerful, confirms our hopes, or comes from someone we respect? YHVH calls us to test what we hear against Scripture rather than follow a speaker blindly.

We should avoid making overconfident claims. Humility means distinguishing Scripture from our understanding of it and admitting when something is our own belief rather than a confirmed word from YHVH.

Disagreement alone does not prove that someone is a false prophet. We must examine the claim, its context, and the evidence, correcting error fairly and truthfully.

If we have repeated a false or unverified claim, we should correct it. Our trust belongs in YHVH, not in a human messenger.

👐 PRAYER

YHVH, our Elohim, teach us to honour Your truth and never claim that You have spoken what You have not spoken. Forgive us for accepting or repeating unverified claims, and give us humility to acknowledge and correct our mistakes.

Help us test every spiritual message against Your Torah, recognise deception without becoming unjustly suspicious, and speak with wisdom and integrity. Protect Your people from false teaching and manipulation, strengthen those who faithfully proclaim Your Word, and give us discernment and courage in difficult times.

May our words and the meditation of our hearts be acceptable before You, YHVH, our Rock and our Redeemer.

Baruch YHVH. Emet.
