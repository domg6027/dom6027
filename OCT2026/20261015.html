📨 DAVAR LECHEM – Yom Chamishi, Shmini 4, 6028 AA

Shalom, Mitspacha!

⭐ THEME

Justice – truth over charisma

📘 READING

“You shall not show partiality in judgment. You shall hear the small as well as the great. You shall not be afraid of the face of any man, for the judgment belongs to Elohim. And the case that is too hard for you, bring to me, and I shall hear it.”
- Devarim (Deuteronomy) 1:17

“You shall not follow a crowd to do evil, nor shall you bear witness in a dispute so as to turn aside after many to pervert justice.”
- Shemot (Exodus) 23:2

“False scales are an abomination to YHVH, but a perfect weight is His delight.”
- Mishlei (Proverbs) 11:1

🟫 MITSVOT FOCUS

Justice must be governed by truth, not by a person's charm, reputation, confidence, popularity, or ability to influence others. Someone may speak persuasively, appear spiritually impressive, or command the loyalty of many people and still be wrong. Charisma is not proof of righteousness, and influence does not establish truth.

Devarim commands us not to show partiality in judgment or to fear the face of any person. The truth must be weighed fairly, whether it comes from someone powerful or someone overlooked. We must not allow status, personal affection, intimidation, or admiration to determine whose words we believe.

Shemot warns us not to follow a crowd when doing so would lead us into wrongdoing or distort justice. Agreement among many people does not automatically make a claim true. When a popular teacher, respected leader, or persuasive speaker departs from YHVH's instructions, loyalty to YHVH must take precedence over loyalty to that person.

Mishlei reminds us that YHVH delights in honest weights. The principle applies not only to trade but also to the way we assess people and their claims. We must use a consistent measure, examine the evidence honestly, and refuse to change our judgment to favour someone we admire or to condemn someone we dislike.

🩵 REFLECTION

When we listen to a persuasive speaker, do we examine the truth of the message, or are we carried along by the speaker's confidence, personality, reputation, or following? Do we apply the same standard to those we admire as we apply to those with whom we disagree?

Justice requires us to distinguish between a person's presentation and the truth of what is being said. We should examine claims against Torah, consider the relevant Scriptural context, and avoid reaching conclusions merely because others have accepted them. Neither popularity nor opposition is a substitute for honest examination.

We must also be willing to acknowledge the truth when it challenges our preferences. A humble person can recognise sound instruction even when it comes from someone unimpressive, and can reject falsehood even when it comes from someone admired. Our responsibility is not to defend personalities but to remain faithful to YHVH's truth.

Today, let us measure words and actions honestly. May we refuse partiality, resist the pressure to follow the crowd, and choose truth over charisma in our judgments and decisions.

👐 PRAYER

YHVH, give us discernment to recognise truth beyond appearances and courage to reject partiality and deception. Help us judge fairly, examine every claim honestly, and remain faithful to Your instructions rather than being swayed by popularity or influence.

Guide our words and decisions in righteousness.

Baruch YHVH. Emet.
