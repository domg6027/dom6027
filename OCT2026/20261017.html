🤲🏻 OPENING PRAYER

Heavenly Father, YHVH our Elohim, we come before You on this Shabbat with grateful hearts. Thank You for bringing us through another week and granting us this set-apart time to rest, reflect, learn and draw nearer to You. Thank You for Your Torah, which teaches us to recognise truth, reject deception and walk faithfully in Your ways.

As we consider the importance of discernment in hearing, prepare our hearts to receive Your instruction with humility. Help us to distinguish between words that come from Your revealed truth and words that arise from human imagination, personal ambition or deception. Give us ears that listen carefully, minds that examine honestly and hearts that are willing to obey what You have commanded.

As we enter Week 44, with its theme of tears and hope, remind us through the words of Yirmeyahu and Eikhah that grief and suffering do not make faithfulness meaningless. The tears of those who have witnessed destruction, injustice and disobedience are not hidden from You. Teach us to acknowledge the seriousness of wrongdoing while holding firmly to the hope that Your compassion and faithfulness remain.

Help us not to become hardened by disappointment or misled by words that promise peace without repentance. Give us courage to face the truth, patience to endure difficult seasons and wisdom to recognise the difference between genuine comfort and false reassurance.

Prepare us to remember Yahusha HaMashiach and the covenant represented through His body and blood. May this Shabbat strengthen our discernment, deepen our trust in You and renew our commitment to live according to Your Word.

Baruch YHVH. Emet.

🍷 COMMUNION

As we partake of the bread and the cup, we remember Yahusha HaMashiach and the covenant represented through His body and blood. Let this remembrance bring us to examine the relationship between what we hear, what we believe and how we live. Covenant faithfulness requires more than listening to words that sound spiritual or comforting. It calls us to receive truth with humility and to demonstrate our faith through obedience.

As we remember Yahusha HaMashiach, let us consider whether our own words bring truth, correction, comfort and hope. May we refuse to manipulate others through spiritual language, and may we never use another person's suffering to justify false promises or careless judgment.

May this remembrance strengthen our commitment to hear faithfully, speak truthfully and walk in obedience to YHVH.

📖 MESSAGE

Discernment in hearing begins with understanding that listening is not the same as accepting everything we hear. YHVH calls His people to hear His instructions, examine what is presented to them and respond in obedience. We must not allow persuasive speech, impressive appearances, popularity or claims of spiritual authority to replace the careful examination of truth.

Devarim warns that even when a sign or wonder comes to pass, we must not follow a messenger who seeks to lead us away from YHVH. An extraordinary event does not give a person permission to contradict the Torah. Neither does a confident declaration establish that a message has come from YHVH. The teaching must be examined according to the covenant and the instructions He has already revealed.

This requires us to distinguish between the appearance of spiritual power and genuine faithfulness. A person may speak with great confidence, attract a large following or offer explanations that seem to answer every question. Yet the central question remains: does the message direct people towards obedience to YHVH, or does it lead them away from His ways?

We must not accept a message merely because the speaker appears sincere, carries an impressive title or claims divine authority. Neither should we reject sound instruction simply because it challenges our preferences. We are responsible for examining what we hear in the light of YHVH's revealed instructions.

The covenant also reminds us that discernment must be accompanied by humility and compassion. We are not called to become suspicious of everyone or to treat every disagreement as proof of deception. We are called to listen carefully, test claims fairly and remain faithful to the truth.

Yirmeyahu (Jeremiah) 23:16–22 exposes the danger of listening to prophets who speak visions from their own hearts rather than from the mouth of YHVH. Their words may sound reassuring, especially to people who are tired, frightened or burdened by difficult circumstances. They may promise peace while refusing to confront wrongdoing. Such reassurance can become a form of deception when it encourages people to remain in disobedience rather than return to YHVH.

Discernment therefore requires more than asking whether a message makes us feel better. We must ask whether it is true, whether it agrees with the Torah and whether it calls us towards righteousness. Genuine comfort does not require the concealment of truth. Genuine correction does not seek to destroy hope. YHVH's instructions expose wrongdoing so that His people can turn from it and walk faithfully.

Yahusha HaMashiach also warned that false prophets may come in sheep's clothing, while their fruit reveals their true character. This teaches us to examine more than a person's presentation. We must consider the substance of the teaching, the conduct of the teacher and the consequences of following that instruction. Words about holiness cannot excuse dishonest conduct, and claims of compassion cannot justify leading people away from YHVH's commands.

At the same time, discernment must not become an excuse for reckless accusations. Not everyone who makes a mistake is a false prophet, and not every disagreement proves that someone is deliberately deceptive. We must examine claims fairly, consider their context and distinguish between an honest misunderstanding that can be corrected and teaching that persistently contradicts YHVH's revealed instructions. We must apply the same standard to those we admire and those we distrust.

The weekly theme, tears and hope, reminds us why this discernment matters so deeply. Yirmeyahu witnessed the consequences of rebellion, while Eikhah gives voice to the grief and devastation that followed Jerusalem's destruction. These writings do not minimise suffering or pretend that disobedience carries no consequences. They teach us to face grief honestly and recognise the seriousness of turning away from YHVH.

Yet the message of Eikhah also declares that YHVH's compassions do not fail and that His faithfulness is great. Hope is not built on denying the truth, making careless promises or pretending that suffering does not exist. Hope rests in the character of YHVH, whose faithfulness remains even when His people pass through painful consequences.

This connection between discernment and hope is important. When we are grieving, we may be especially vulnerable to anyone who promises an immediate answer or claims to possess secret knowledge of what YHVH will do. We must not let our longing for relief make us careless with the truth. Nor should we allow disappointment to persuade us that YHVH has abandoned us. We can acknowledge our pain honestly while continuing to trust His character and remain faithful to His instructions.

The words we choose to hear also shape the words we pass on to others. If we accept false reassurance, we may repeat it to those who need honest guidance. If we accept accusations without examining the evidence, we may contribute to injustice. If we listen only to those who confirm our opinions, we may become unable to recognise correction. Faithful hearing therefore carries responsibility for how we speak, teach and influence others.

Let us learn to listen with humility rather than defensiveness. When instruction is supported by YHVH's Word, we should be willing to receive it even when it challenges us. When a claim is uncertain, we should be honest about that uncertainty rather than presenting our own interpretation as a direct command from YHVH. When a teaching contradicts His instructions, we must have the courage to reject it, regardless of the speaker's reputation or following.

Shabbat gives us an opportunity to become still enough to examine what has been shaping our thoughts. Which voices have we trusted? Have we tested their words, or have we accepted them because they were familiar, comforting or confidently delivered? Have we allowed fear, grief or the desire for approval to weaken our discernment? Are we prepared to receive truth even when it calls for repentance or a change of direction?

May we become people who hear carefully, test honestly and respond faithfully. May our discernment protect us from deception without making us hard-hearted. May our hope remain rooted in YHVH's faithfulness rather than in promises that He has not given. And may our words and actions reflect the truth we profess, even through seasons of grief and uncertainty.

🙏🏻 CLOSING PRAYER

YHVH our Elohim, thank You for Your Torah and for the discernment You teach us through Your Word. Forgive us for the times we have accepted words without examining them, trusted appearances more than truth or allowed fear and the desire for comfort to influence our judgment. Give us wisdom to recognise deception, humility to receive correction and courage to reject teaching that leads us away from Your instructions. Guard us against manipulation, false reassurance and claims of authority that do not reflect Your revealed truth.

When we hear words that sound impressive, teach us to examine their substance. When we encounter teachings that challenge us, help us to test them fairly rather than react with pride or defensiveness. Keep us from making careless accusations, and give us the patience to distinguish honest mistakes from persistent deception. May our hearing, understanding, speech and conduct be governed by reverence for You and faithfulness to Your Torah.

YHVH, remember those who are grieving, those who have witnessed destruction and those who carry sorrow that others cannot see. As we reflect on the tears recorded in Yirmeyahu and Eikhah, help us to acknowledge suffering without surrendering hope. Comfort those who mourn, strengthen those who are weary and guide those who are searching for understanding. Remind us that Your compassions do not fail and that Your faithfulness remains even in painful seasons. Lead us away from false comfort and towards the hope grounded in Your character and truth.

We pray that You strengthen families to build relationships founded upon honesty, faithfulness, respect and responsible conduct. Protect marriages from betrayal and households from deception, manipulation and division.

We pray that You give parents wisdom to teach their children the value of truth and loyalty. Help children to grow in discernment, integrity and respect for the responsibilities entrusted to them.

We pray that You protect Your people among the nations. Give wisdom to those who hold positions of authority, and restrain those who seek to use power for oppression, deception or destruction.

We pray that You strengthen those who must make difficult decisions for the protection of others. Give them the courage of Esther, the loyalty of Mordekai and the wisdom to act at the right time.

We pray for peace in the Middle East and for the protection of innocent people affected by war and violence. Strengthen and comfort the elderly, protect farmers and those whose livelihoods depend upon the land, and watch over people facing war, disaster and displacement.

We pray that You strengthen and protect evangelists, teachers and missionaries who faithfully proclaim Your Word. Give them wisdom, protection and endurance.

We pray that You bring healing and comfort to those who are ill, suffering or afraid. Prepare Your people for the coming tribulation and teach us to remain faithful under pressure.

We pray that You bless and strengthen Derech Olam Mishkan International. Help us to serve faithfully, preserve truth, encourage Your people and remain steadfast in Your covenant.

Bless this Shabbat with rest, understanding and renewed commitment. May we leave this set-apart time more discerning in what we hear, more compassionate towards those who suffer and more steadfast in the hope that rests in Your faithfulness.

Baruch YHVH. Emet.
