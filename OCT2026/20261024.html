Thank You for Your Torah, which teaches us to distinguish between truth and deception, wisdom and foolishness, and faithful obedience and the pursuit of forbidden knowledge.

Teach us not to confuse coincidence with divine direction, superstition with wisdom, or impressive claims with truth. Renew our hearts, strengthen our willingness to repent and help us respond faithfully when You expose what needs to change.

Prepare us to remember Yahusha HaMashiach and the covenant represented through His body and blood. May this Shabbat deepen our understanding, strengthen our discernment and renew our commitment to live according to Your Torah.

🍷 COMMUNION

As we partake of the bread and the cup, we remember Yahusha HaMashiach and the covenant represented through His body and blood. This remembrance calls us to examine our lives honestly before YHVH. Covenant faithfulness is not established by outward appearances, spiritual claims or rituals performed without understanding. It is demonstrated through a life that seeks truth, practises righteousness and responds to YHVH's instructions with obedience.

Superstition often grows where fear and uncertainty are allowed to govern our decisions. People may seek signs, secret knowledge, lucky objects or supposed spiritual techniques in an attempt to control circumstances or discover what lies ahead. Yet the covenant calls us to trust YHVH rather than attempt to manipulate the future. We must not replace His revealed instructions with practices that He has forbidden.

As we remember Yahusha HaMashiach, let us examine whether our decisions are governed by wisdom or by fear. Are we willing to accept responsibility for our actions, or do we seek excuses, blame others or rely on beliefs that promise protection without requiring righteousness? Do we trust YHVH enough to obey Him when the outcome is uncertain?

May this remembrance strengthen our commitment to reject superstition, pursue wisdom, take responsibility for our conduct and walk faithfully before YHVH.

📖 MESSAGE

Justice requires us to recognise the difference between genuine wisdom and beliefs that lead people away from truth. YHVH does not call His people to make decisions through superstition, divination or attempts to obtain forbidden knowledge. He calls us to walk in reverence, examine our conduct and seek understanding through the instructions He has revealed.

Superstition often offers an attractive promise: certainty without patience, protection without obedience, or knowledge without the responsibility of testing what we believe. It may appear harmless when expressed through familiar sayings, repeated rituals or personal interpretations of events. Yet when superstition begins to govern our choices, it can replace trust in YHVH with fear of signs, objects, numbers, circumstances or supposed hidden forces.

The Torah gives clear boundaries concerning divination and other forbidden practices. These instructions are not invitations to live in constant fear of the unseen. They teach us that we must not seek spiritual knowledge through means YHVH has prohibited. We are responsible for the practices we choose, the beliefs we cultivate and the influence those beliefs have on our conduct.

We must therefore distinguish between wisdom and superstition. Wisdom examines circumstances carefully, considers evidence, seeks righteous counsel and measures decisions against YHVH's revealed instructions. Superstition often assigns hidden meaning to events without a sound basis, encourages fear or claims certainty where none has been given. Wisdom accepts that we do not know everything. Superstition may tempt us to believe that we can discover or control what has not been revealed.

This does not mean that we should dismiss every unusual event or refuse to consider the lessons that circumstances may teach us. It means that we must not automatically declare an event to be a sign from YHVH or build a doctrine upon our personal interpretation. We should be honest about what we know, what we do not know and what the Scriptures actually establish. A sincere feeling, an unexpected coincidence or a compelling story does not become divine instruction simply because it affects us deeply.

Mishlei teaches that the prudent person considers their steps rather than believing every word. This principle applies to spiritual claims, popular teachings, predictions and advice. We must not accept a claim merely because someone speaks confidently, presents an impressive testimony or insists that a particular event confirms their interpretation. We must examine the claim carefully and determine whether it agrees with YHVH's instructions.

Justice also requires consistency. We should not scrutinise the claims of those we dislike while accepting every statement made by those we admire. Nor should we treat our own interpretations as unquestionable simply because they seem reasonable to us. If we expect others to test their beliefs, we must be willing to test our own. Humility allows us to acknowledge when we have misunderstood something, accepted an unfounded belief or drawn a conclusion that the evidence does not support.

There is an important difference between asking YHVH for wisdom and attempting to obtain forbidden knowledge. Prayer acknowledges our dependence upon Him. Studying Torah teaches us the boundaries of righteous conduct. Seeking sound counsel helps us consider matters we may have overlooked. Careful examination allows us to make responsible decisions. None of these requires us to manipulate circumstances or seek secret knowledge through prohibited spiritual practices.

Trusting YHVH does not mean abandoning practical judgment. A person who needs to make a difficult decision should consider the facts, seek appropriate advice, examine the likely consequences and act responsibly. We should not neglect ordinary responsibilities while waiting for an extraordinary sign. Faithfulness often requires us to apply the wisdom already given to us.

The weekly theme of responsibility and renewal takes this teaching further. Through Yechezqel, YHVH confronts the tendency to avoid personal accountability. The people could not excuse their own conduct indefinitely by appealing to the actions of others. Each person was called to recognise wrongdoing, turn away from it and walk in what was right.

This principle remains essential in our own lives. We cannot blame superstition, tradition, family history or the influence of others for every decision we make. Although our circumstances and upbringing may affect us, we remain responsible for examining what we believe and choosing how we respond. When we discover that a practice is contrary to YHVH's instructions, we must not defend it merely because it is familiar or because we have followed it for many years.

Repentance begins with honesty. We must be willing to name wrongdoing for what it is, abandon what YHVH has prohibited and correct our conduct. It is not enough to recognise that a belief may be mistaken while continuing to organise our lives around it. Genuine repentance changes direction. It moves us away from what is false and towards what is faithful.

Yet YHVH's call to responsibility is not without hope. Yechezqel speaks of a new heart and a new spirit. Renewal is not simply an outward adjustment intended to make us appear more righteous. It involves becoming responsive to YHVH's instruction and allowing His Ru’ach HaQodesh to guide us towards faithful obedience.

A heart of stone resists correction. It becomes defensive, justifies wrongdoing and refuses to reconsider established habits. A responsive heart is willing to listen, examine, repent and change. Such renewal does not make us incapable of error, but it changes how we respond when our error is exposed. Instead of protecting our pride, we seek truth. Instead of making excuses, we accept responsibility. Instead of remaining bound to familiar practices, we turn towards obedience.

We must also remember that responsibility does not mean assuming blame for everything that happens to us. We are accountable for our own conduct, not automatically guilty of every hardship, loss or injustice we experience. Wisdom distinguishes between personal wrongdoing, the wrongdoing of others and circumstances beyond our control. Justice requires us to make those distinctions carefully rather than blaming victims or using suffering as evidence of hidden guilt.

Likewise, we must not use the language of renewal to manipulate others. We can encourage people to examine their conduct and return to YHVH's instructions, but we should not claim authority over another person's conscience or declare every personal opinion to be a divine command. Correction must remain truthful, responsible and consistent with the character of the One whose ways we seek to uphold.

The connection between justice, wisdom and renewal is therefore clear. We cannot walk in justice while allowing unfounded beliefs to govern our judgments. We cannot grow in wisdom while refusing to examine our assumptions. And we cannot experience meaningful renewal while protecting practices that we know must be abandoned.

This Shabbat offers us an opportunity to consider the beliefs and habits that influence our daily decisions. Have we allowed fear to make choices for us? Have we treated coincidences as instructions without examining them? Have we accepted claims because they promised certainty or comfort? Have we blamed others instead of acknowledging our own responsibility? Are we willing to change when YHVH's Word reveals that we have been mistaken?

Let us become people who seek wisdom rather than secret knowledge, who choose truth rather than convenient explanations, and who accept responsibility rather than hiding behind excuses. Let us reject forbidden practices without becoming fearful of every unfamiliar circumstance. Let us examine our beliefs with humility, act with sound judgment and remain willing to receive correction.

May YHVH renew our hearts so that our conduct reflects the truth we profess. May justice govern our decisions, wisdom guide our steps and obedience shape our lives. May we learn to trust YHVH without attempting to control what He has not revealed, and may our renewal become evident through faithful choices, honest relationships and righteous conduct.

🙏🏻 CLOSING PRAYER

YHVH our Elohim, we thank You for Your Torah, which gives us clear instruction and protects us from paths that lead to deception. Forgive us for the times we have allowed fear, superstition, human tradition or unfounded claims to influence our decisions. Forgive us when we have accepted something without examining it, attributed meanings to events without a sound basis or sought certainty through practices You have forbidden.

Give us wisdom to distinguish truth from speculation. Teach us to examine claims carefully, to seek righteous counsel and to recognise the limits of our own understanding. Guard us against divination, sorcery, superstition and every practice that draws us away from trusting You. Help us not to replace obedience with rituals or sound judgment with fear. May our confidence rest in Your character and in the instructions You have revealed.

YHVH, teach us to accept responsibility for our conduct. Where we have done wrong, give us the humility to acknowledge it, the courage to repent and the determination to make the necessary changes. Where we have blamed others unfairly, correct our hearts. Where we have defended familiar habits rather than examining them, open our understanding. Give us hearts that are responsive to Your instruction and willing to walk in Your statutes.

We ask You to renew us inwardly. Remove the hardness that resists correction and cultivate within us a sincere desire to live faithfully. Let Your Ru’ach HaQodesh guide us towards obedience, integrity and discernment. May our renewal be visible not merely in our words, but in our decisions, our relationships and the way we treat others. Help us to distinguish between genuine personal responsibility and blame that is unfairly placed upon those who suffer.

We pray for those who have been deceived, exploited or frightened by false spiritual claims. Bring them understanding and freedom from fear. Give them wise and trustworthy people who can help them examine what they have been taught. Protect those who are vulnerable to manipulation, and give those who teach or advise others the humility to speak truthfully rather than claim knowledge or authority You have not given them.

We pray that You strengthen families to build relationships founded upon honesty, faithfulness, respect and responsible conduct. Protect marriages from betrayal and households from deception, manipulation and division.

We pray that You give parents wisdom to teach their children the value of truth and loyalty. Help children to grow in discernment, integrity and respect for the responsibilities entrusted to them.

We pray that You protect Your people among the nations. Give wisdom to those who hold positions of authority, and restrain those who seek to use power for oppression, deception or destruction.

We pray that You strengthen those who must make difficult decisions for the protection of others. Give them the courage of Esther, the loyalty of Mordekai and the wisdom to act at the right time.

We pray for peace in the Middle East and for the protection of innocent people affected by war and violence. Strengthen and comfort the elderly, protect farmers and those whose livelihoods depend upon the land, and watch over people facing war, disaster and displacement. Give wisdom to those working to protect life, relieve suffering and pursue justice.

We pray that You strengthen and protect evangelists, teachers and missionaries who faithfully proclaim Your Word. Give them wisdom, protection and endurance. Keep them from error, pride and the temptation to use spiritual influence irresponsibly. Help them to teach faithfully, live with integrity and direct people towards obedience to Your revealed instructions.

We pray that You bring healing and comfort to those who are ill, suffering or afraid. Strengthen those who care for them, provide for those who lack essential needs and comfort those who are grieving. Prepare Your people for the coming tribulation and teach us to remain faithful under pressure, refusing deception and standing firmly in Your truth.

We pray that You bless and strengthen Derech Olam Mishkan International. Help us to serve faithfully, preserve truth, encourage Your people and remain steadfast in Your covenant. Give wisdom to those entrusted with responsibility, protect our work from deception and division, and help us to correct our mistakes with humility. May everything we teach and practise be tested against Your Word and directed towards faithful obedience.

As we rest on this Shabbat, quiet our anxious thoughts and renew our understanding. Help us to release the desire to control what You have not revealed and to concentrate on the responsibilities You have placed before us. May this set-apart day strengthen our trust, deepen our wisdom and prepare us to walk faithfully in the days ahead.

Bless this Shabbat with rest, peace and renewed commitment. May our hearts remain responsive to Your instruction, our judgments be governed by justice and our lives bear witness to the wisdom that comes from walking in Your ways.

Baruch YHVH. Emet.
