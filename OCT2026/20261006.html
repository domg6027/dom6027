Shalom, Mitspacha!

⭐ THEME

Justice – guarding spiritual purity

📘 READING

“You shall not pervert justice. You shall not show partiality to the poor or favour the great, but in righteousness you shall judge your neighbour.”
- Vayiqra (Leviticus) 19:15

“Learn to do good; seek justice, correct oppression; bring justice to the fatherless, plead the widow’s cause.”
- Yeshayahu (Isaiah) 1:17

“Beloved, do not imitate what is evil, but what is good. He who does good is of Elohim, but he who does evil has not seen Elohim.”
- 3 Yochanan (3 John) 1:11

🟫 MITSVOT FOCUS

Justice before YHVH is not merely a matter of making correct decisions in public situations. It also requires us to guard the purity of our own hearts, motives and conduct. YHVH does not separate righteousness from the way we treat others.

The Torah forbids partiality because human judgement can easily be corrupted by preference, status, wealth, weakness or personal relationships. A person may appear righteous while quietly allowing prejudice, resentment or favouritism to influence their decisions.

Guarding spiritual purity therefore requires us to examine what is taking place within us before we presume to judge others. We cannot faithfully pursue justice while permitting injustice, dishonesty or partiality to remain hidden in our own hearts.

YHVH calls us to pursue what is good, to correct what is wrong and to defend those who are vulnerable. This requires both courage and self-discipline. Justice is not vindictiveness, and spiritual purity is not pretending that wrongdoing does not exist. Both require us to stand firmly with truth while refusing to corrupt our judgement through hatred, pride or personal advantage.

The mitsvah therefore reaches beyond outward behaviour. We are called to become people whose decisions, relationships and motives are increasingly governed by the righteousness of YHVH.

🩵 REFLECTION

Guarding spiritual purity begins with honest self-examination.

It is possible to speak strongly about justice while being selective about the people to whom we apply it. We may demand truth from others while excusing dishonesty in ourselves, condemn partiality while showing favouritism, or defend righteousness only when it benefits people we already support.

YHVH's standard does not change according to our preferences.

As we return to ordinary days after the Mo'edim of 6028 AA, this becomes part of the test of what we have learned. The appointed days have reminded us of YHVH, His Torah and His ways, but ordinary days reveal whether those lessons have become part of our daily walk.

Let us therefore guard the purity of our hearts as carefully as we guard our outward conduct. Let us examine our motives before YHVH, judge matters according to truth rather than personal preference, and refuse to allow resentment, pride or favouritism to distort our understanding of justice.

May our pursuit of justice begin with faithfulness in the places where YHVH has placed us. When our hearts are governed by His truth, our treatment of others can increasingly reflect His righteousness.

👐 PRAYER

YHVH our Elohim, search our hearts and reveal anything within us that could corrupt our judgement or compromise our spiritual purity.

Keep us from partiality, prejudice, resentment and pride. Teach us to distinguish between righteous judgement and personal vengeance, and between genuine justice and the desire to see others condemned.

Help us to examine our own conduct before we judge the conduct of others. Give us courage to correct what is wrong, humility to receive correction ourselves, and wisdom to act according to Your Torah.

Guard our hearts from motives that would distort truth. Keep us from favouring the powerful, overlooking wrongdoing because it is convenient, or treating others according to our personal feelings rather than according to righteousness.

As we continue in the ordinary days following the Mo'edim of 6028 AA, let what You have taught us become evident in our daily choices. May our words, decisions and relationships reflect justice, mercy and faithfulness before You.

Keep us spiritually pure, faithful to Your Torah and steadfast in truth.

Baruch YHVH. Emet.
