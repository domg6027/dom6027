📨 DAVAR LECHEM – Yom Revi'i, Chamishi 14, 6028 AA

Shalom, Mitspacha!

⭐ THEME

Moral Courage in Crisis

📘 READING

“Rescue those being led away to death, and hold back those staggering toward slaughter.”
- Mishlei (Proverbs) 24:11

“Open your mouth, judge righteously, and defend the cause of the poor and needy.”
- Mishlei (Proverbs) 31:9

“Do not be overcome by evil, but overcome evil with good.”
- Romiyim (Romans) 12:21

🟫 MITZVOT FOCUS

YHVH calls His people to demonstrate moral courage when others are vulnerable and circumstances are difficult. Righteousness is not merely a private conviction; it requires us to act when truth, justice and the protection of others demand a response. Courage means choosing what is right even when remaining silent would be easier or more comfortable.

This mitzvah reminds us that faithfulness requires both discernment and action. We must not allow fear, pressure or uncertainty to prevent us from defending what is right, assisting those in need and standing firmly for truth according to YHVH's standards.

🩵 REFLECTION

Crises often reveal what is truly established within our hearts. When circumstances become difficult, it can be tempting to protect our own comfort and leave difficult matters to others. Yet YHVH calls His people to remain faithful even when doing what is right requires courage.

May we develop hearts that respond to crisis with wisdom rather than panic, courage rather than fear and compassion rather than indifference. As we faithfully stand for truth and serve those in need, our lives can become witnesses of YHVH's righteousness in difficult times.

👐 PRAYER

Heavenly Father, YHVH, thank You for teaching us to stand firmly for what is right. Strengthen our hearts when circumstances become difficult, give us wisdom to discern Your will and courage to act according to Your righteousness. Keep us from fear, indifference or compromise, and help us to protect, encourage and serve those who need our help. May our lives faithfully reflect Your truth, justice and mercy in every crisis.

Baruch YHVH. Emet.

Shalom until tomorrow.
