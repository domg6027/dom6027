📖 MESSAGE

PROHIBITION OF BESTIALITY

The Torah establishes a clear boundary between human and animal life. YHVH created human beings and animals according to their distinct orders, and His instructions concerning sexual conduct protect that distinction from being violated.

The prohibition of bestiality demonstrates that human desire does not determine what is permitted. YHVH determines the boundaries of righteous conduct.

Human beings have been given authority within creation, but authority does not mean unrestricted freedom. Responsibility accompanies authority. We are called to exercise dominion according to the order established by YHVH rather than treating creation as something available for every expression of human desire.

The seriousness of this boundary also reminds us that animals cannot participate in human covenantal relationships or provide human consent. They belong to a different order of creation and must not be subjected to human sexual conduct.

When a boundary is established by YHVH, obedience requires that we respect it even when the surrounding culture may redefine what is acceptable.

The Torah places this boundary within the wider principle of holiness. YHVH commanded Yisra’el not to defile themselves with the practices of the nations around them. His people were to remain distinct in conduct because they belonged to Him.

This distinction does not give us permission to treat others with contempt. Instead, it calls us to examine ourselves. We are responsible for our own obedience.

We must therefore be careful not to become so focused on the sins of others that we fail to examine our own hearts. The same Torah that establishes boundaries for conduct also calls us to walk humbly before YHVH.

Where temptation exists, wisdom requires distance from what can lead to transgression. Where harmful behaviour has occurred, protection and appropriate intervention are necessary. Where someone has sinned, repentance requires truth rather than concealment.

The holiness of creation is not preserved by pretending that violations do not occur. It is preserved by recognising what YHVH established, refusing to normalise what He prohibited, protecting what is vulnerable, and returning to righteousness when we have gone astray.

WORSHIP AND REPENTANCE

This is where the weekly theme of worship and repentance meets today's daily theme. True worship requires more than declaring reverence for YHVH while disregarding His instructions. Worship is expressed through obedience, and repentance is the turning away from what violates His ways.

The Psalms repeatedly call us to approach YHVH with honest hearts. Worship includes praise, but it also includes confession, humility and a willingness to be corrected.

Repentance is therefore not merely regret after wrongdoing. It is a turning of the heart and conduct back toward the order established by YHVH.

When Torah reveals a boundary, we have a choice. We can defend our desires, follow the standards of the surrounding world, or humble ourselves and return to what YHVH has commanded.

The awakening of Yom Teru'ah gives this truth particular weight. The shofar calls attention to the King. It reminds us that we are not the ultimate authority over our lives. YHVH is King, and His Torah teaches us how to live before Him.

Worship without obedience becomes empty expression. Repentance without changed direction remains incomplete. But when the heart turns back to YHVH and the life follows that turning, worship becomes a lived response to the King.

The worship described throughout the Psalms is not detached from the reality of human failure. YHVH is praised as the One who hears the brokenhearted, forgives those who turn to Him, and teaches His people His ways.

A repentant heart does not redefine sin to make it comfortable. It acknowledges what is wrong and seeks a return to obedience.

That return may require changes in behaviour, boundaries, relationships and habits. It may require refusing influences that continually pull us away from Torah. It may require seeking protection or accountability where serious wrongdoing has occurred.

Worship therefore reaches into the practical details of life. The way we use our bodies, the way we treat other living beings, the way we respond to temptation, and the way we respond when Torah corrects us are all part of our worship before YHVH.

As we enter another Shabbat, let us remember that repentance is not humiliation for its own sake. It is the pathway back to righteousness. YHVH calls His people to return to Him, not to remain trapped in rebellion.

May our worship be sincere. May our repentance be genuine. May our conduct reflect the holiness we profess.

And may the call of Yom Teru'ah continue to awaken us to the reality that YHVH is King over every area of life.

🌙 YOM TERU'AH DAY 7: Awakening and kingship

Yom Teru'ah calls us to awaken and recognise the kingship of YHVH. The sounding of the shofar is a call to attention: to hear, to remember, and to respond.

On this seventh day, the call to awakening reminds us that YHVH's kingship includes the boundaries He established throughout creation. We do not determine the order of creation according to our desires; we receive His instruction and respond with obedience.

The call of the King therefore leads naturally into repentance. When we recognise that we have crossed a boundary, we are called to turn back. When we recognise His order, we are called to honour it.

May this appointed time awaken us to worship that is expressed through obedience and repentance that produces a genuine return to the ways of YHVH.

🤲🏻 CLOSING PRAYER

Heavenly Father, YHVH, thank You for bringing us through Yom Teru'ah Day 7 and for allowing us to enter this Double Shabbat with hearts turned toward You.

Thank You for Your Torah, which gives us clear boundaries and teaches us to respect the order You established throughout creation.

Forgive us where we have allowed desire, culture, curiosity or human reasoning to influence us beyond the boundaries You established. Give us humility to acknowledge wrongdoing and courage to turn away from it.

Teach us to worship You with our whole lives. Let our praise be accompanied by obedience, our repentance by genuine change, and our knowledge of Your Torah by faithful conduct.

Protect animals from cruelty, exploitation and every misuse of the authority human beings have been given over them.

Protect children and vulnerable people from exposure to sexual corruption and from adults who would exploit their vulnerability.

Give wisdom to parents, caregivers and communities to establish appropriate boundaries and to protect those entrusted to their care.

We pray for those who have been exposed to sexual wrongdoing or exploitation. Bring truth into what has been hidden, protection where protection is needed, and appropriate justice where wrongdoing has occurred.

We pray for those struggling with desires or behaviours that conflict with Your Torah. Give them courage to seek help, establish righteous boundaries and turn away from what is harmful and forbidden.

We pray for those who need repentance. Soften hearts without compromising truth, and lead those who have sinned toward genuine confession, changed conduct and restoration where restoration is possible.

Teach us not to hide behind religious appearance while refusing correction. Let our worship be honest before You.

As the shofar calls us to awaken, keep us attentive to the voice of the King. Let the worship of this Shabbat lead us toward repentance, and let repentance lead us back into faithful obedience.

May our lives honour the order You established. May our conduct reflect holiness. May our worship be sincere.

Baruch YHVH. Emet.
