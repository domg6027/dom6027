⭐ THEME

Justice – preserving created order

🤲🏻 OPENING PRAYER

Heavenly Father, YHVH, we come before You on Yom Teru'ah Day 9 with grateful hearts. Thank You for carrying us through these days of awakening and for teaching us through Your Torah to recognise the order You established within creation.

Thank You for calling us to justice, responsibility and faithful obedience. Teach us to understand that preserving Your created order is not merely about recognising boundaries, but about living responsibly within them.

Examine our hearts where we have disregarded Your order, neglected responsibilities or allowed human desires and reasoning to replace Your instruction.

Give us wisdom to recognise where justice requires protection, correction, accountability and restoration. Teach us to act with righteousness without compromising truth.

As Yom Teru'ah reaches its final day, keep us attentive to the call of the King. May the awakening of these days prepare our hearts to turn toward You with greater humility and obedience.

And as we approach Erev Yom Kippur, prepare us to examine ourselves honestly before You.

Baruch YHVH. Emet.

🍷 COMMUNION

As we partake of the bread and the cup, we remember Yahusha HaMashiach and the covenant represented through His body and blood.

The covenant calls us to walk in holiness, truth and faithful obedience. It reminds us that reconciliation with YHVH cannot be separated from the way we live and from the way we treat those entrusted to our care.

Justice requires more than recognising wrongdoing. It requires us to respond to wrongdoing truthfully, to protect the vulnerable and to accept correction when our own conduct has fallen short.

As we remember Yahusha HaMashiach, may we examine whether our lives reflect the righteousness we profess.

May this remembrance prepare us to enter Erev Yom Kippur with honest hearts, willing to recognise where we have failed, to turn from wrongdoing and to seek restoration where restoration is possible.

📖 MESSAGE

The final day of Yom Teru'ah brings together the themes of awakening, kingship, justice and responsibility.

Throughout these days, the Torah has called us to recognise boundaries that protect the order YHVH established. Today the focus turns toward justice as a means of preserving that order.

Justice is not merely punishment after wrongdoing. Justice requires that we recognise what is right, protect what is vulnerable, correct what is wrong and refuse to allow injustice to become normal.

The Torah does not permit justice to be altered according to status, wealth, popularity or the pressure of a crowd. Righteous judgment requires faithfulness to what YHVH has established.

This means that preserving created order also requires responsible human conduct. We cannot claim to honour YHVH's order while ignoring injustice within our relationships, families and communities.

Justice therefore begins with truth.

Where wrongdoing has occurred, truth must not be replaced by concealment. Where someone has been harmed, their vulnerability must not be ignored simply because acknowledging the harm is uncomfortable.

At the same time, justice requires us to examine ourselves. We cannot demand righteousness from others while refusing correction when Torah exposes our own failures.

The call of Yom Teru'ah is therefore also a call to attention. The shofar awakens us to the reality that YHVH is King.

Because He is King, His standards—not human preference—define righteousness.

The final day of Yom Teru'ah reminds us that awakening must produce response. It is not enough to hear the call. We must allow what we have heard to affect our conduct.

Preserving created order means accepting the responsibilities YHVH has given us. It means respecting boundaries, protecting life, exercising authority responsibly and refusing to participate in conduct that destroys what He established.

Justice also requires courage. There are times when remaining silent allows wrongdoing to continue. There are times when intervention is necessary to protect someone vulnerable. There are times when correction must be given, and times when we ourselves must receive correction.

Righteousness does not mean acting harshly. It means acting truthfully and responsibly.

The Torah repeatedly joins justice with care for those who can easily be overlooked: the stranger, the fatherless, the widow and others who may lack protection or power.

This teaches us that preserving order is not merely about defending structures. It is also about protecting people within those structures.

Yom Teru'ah has awakened us to the kingship of YHVH. Now that awakening must become faithful responsibility.

We are not called to create our own order and then ask YHVH to approve it. We are called to receive His instruction and live according to it.

As we approach Erev Yom Kippur, this becomes even more personal.

The question is not only whether we can identify injustice around us. We must also ask whether there are places where our own conduct, words, decisions or attitudes have contributed to disorder.

Repentance begins when we stop defending ourselves and allow truth to speak.

That does not mean accepting false accusations or carrying guilt for what we did not do. It means being honest about what is actually ours to confess and correct.

Where we have sinned, we must turn back.

Where we have harmed others, we must seek appropriate restoration where possible.

Where boundaries need to be established, we must establish them.

Where accountability is necessary, we must not resist it.

And where forgiveness is possible, we must not allow pride to prevent reconciliation.

The final call of Yom Teru'ah therefore prepares us for the solemnity of Erev Yom Kippur. The King has awakened us. Now we must examine how we have responded.

May we not allow these days to become merely another sequence of appointed-time observances. May the awakening reach our conduct, our relationships, our decisions and our hearts.

Justice preserves what is right. Repentance restores what has been broken. Obedience keeps us within the order YHVH established.

And as we stand on the threshold of Yom Kippur, may we be willing to let YHVH search us, correct us and lead us back into faithful obedience.

🌙 YOM TERU'AH DAY 9: Awakening and kingship

Yom Teru'ah reaches its final day with the call to remain awake to the kingship of YHVH.

The shofar has called us to attention throughout these appointed days. It has reminded us that YHVH is King and that His authority extends over every part of life.

On Day 9, the awakening turns our attention toward justice and the preservation of the order YHVH established.

Justice requires us to recognise what is right, protect what is vulnerable, correct wrongdoing and accept correction when we have gone astray.

May the final day of Yom Teru'ah leave us not merely informed, but awakened. May the call of the King continue to sound within us as we prepare to enter Erev Yom Kippur.

🌙 EREV YOM KIPPUR: Preparation, examination and turning

As Yom Teru'ah comes to its conclusion, we stand at the threshold of Yom Kippur.

Erev Yom Kippur calls us to prepare with seriousness and honesty. The approaching Day of Atonement is not a time for outward appearance without inward examination. It calls us to consider our ways before YHVH.

Preparation begins with truth. We cannot turn from what we refuse to recognise.

Where Torah has exposed wrongdoing, we must not excuse it. Where our conduct has harmed others, we must not hide behind pride or religious appearance. Where correction is needed, we must be willing to receive it.

At the same time, examination must remain righteous. We are not called to manufacture guilt or accept accusations that are untrue. We are called to examine what is actually ours before YHVH.

This is a time to consider our relationship with Him, our conduct toward others, our responsibilities, our boundaries and the ways in which we have responded to His instruction.

May Erev Yom Kippur therefore be a threshold of genuine preparation.

May we enter the coming day with humility rather than self-righteousness, with truth rather than concealment, and with a heart willing to turn back toward YHVH.

The awakening of Yom Teru'ah has prepared us to hear the call.

Now let that awakening lead us toward examination, repentance and a renewed commitment to walk faithfully before the King.

🤲🏻 CLOSING PRAYER

Heavenly Father, YHVH, thank You for bringing us through the nine days of Yom Teru'ah and for awakening us to Your kingship.

Thank You for Your Torah, which teaches us justice, responsibility, boundaries and the preservation of the order You established.

Forgive us where we have acted unjustly, ignored wrongdoing, neglected responsibility or allowed our own desires and reasoning to take precedence over Your instruction.

Search our hearts and reveal what needs correction. Give us humility to receive Your correction and courage to make the changes You require.

Teach us to protect those who are vulnerable, to speak truthfully, to act justly and to exercise authority responsibly.

We pray for families and communities where injustice has caused harm. Bring protection to those who are vulnerable, truth where wrongdoing has been hidden, and appropriate justice where it is required.

We pray for those who are carrying the consequences of wrongdoing committed against them. Bring comfort, protection, wise support and restoration where restoration is possible.

We pray for those who have caused harm. Bring conviction that leads to genuine repentance rather than defensiveness or concealment. Give them courage to acknowledge wrongdoing and to accept appropriate accountability.

As we enter Erev Yom Kippur, prepare our hearts for honest examination.

Keep us from false guilt, but also keep us from self-deception. Teach us to recognise what is truly ours before You and to respond with repentance where we have sinned.

Where reconciliation is possible, give us humility to pursue it. Where boundaries are necessary, give us wisdom to establish them. Where accountability is required, keep us from resisting it.

May the awakening of Yom Teru'ah remain within us as we approach Yom Kippur. Let the call of the King continue to lead us toward truth, justice, repentance and faithful obedience.

Prepare us to enter the coming appointed day with hearts turned toward You.

Baruch YHVH. Emet.
